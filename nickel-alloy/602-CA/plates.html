Alloy 602 CA is a high‑carbon nickel‑chromium‑iron alloy with aluminium and yttrium additions that form a tightly adhering chromium‑aluminium oxide scale, giving extremely low scaling rates at temperature and self‑healing behaviour when the surface is damaged. The high chromium content around 25% combined with approximately 2.2% aluminium and a microalloyed 0.1% yttrium enables a continuous alumina subscale whose adhesion and spall‑resistance are significantly better than conventional heat‑resistant alloys, making Alloy 602 CA a preferred material where product contamination from scale must be minimal, such as calciners and high‑purity rotary kilns.

For procurement heads, process and mechanical engineers, fabricators, OEMs and hardware or industrial supply stores serving chemical, oil & gas, marine, pharma and power generation, Alloy 602 CA plates answer a specific gap: stainless steels and many nickel alloys lose strength, carburise or shed scale in high‑load, cyclic high‑temperature service, whereas 602 CA combines very high creep strength with oxidation and carburisation resistance in methane, carbon monoxide and carbon‑rich gases. Technical notes explain that high‑nickel alloys like 602 CA show excellent resistance to carburisation because the tenacious oxide scale impedes carbon diffusion; creep‑rupture and creep‑limit data confirm that the alloy maintains useful creep resistance at temperatures where many grades have already exhausted design life, supporting its use in heat‑treating baskets, trays, hangers and long‑span radiant tubes.
