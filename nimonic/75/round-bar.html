    Nimonic 75 (UNS N06075, W.Nr. 2.4951 / 2.4630) is an 80/20 nickel-chromium alloy with controlled additions of titanium and carbon, originally developed for gas turbine blades in the 1940s. Its chemistry delivers excellent oxidation and scaling resistance together with medium-to-high strength at temperatures up to about 650–900°C, far beyond the safe range of many stainless grades. This makes it especially valuable in power-plant exhaust systems, industrial furnace muffles and liners, and land-based gas turbine structural components where temperature and flue chemistry punish conventional steels.

    Compared with Inconel 600, Nimonic 75’s higher chromium and tailored titanium-carbon levels give stronger scale adherence and significantly better hot-corrosion behavior in some studies, with up to ~160% improved resistance versus other nickel alloys in aggressive environments. Because of its composition and thermal conductivity characteristics, Nimonic 75 can also transfer heat slightly differently than Inconel 600, improving stability of furnace internals in continuous-duty lines. For procurement teams balancing capex and downtime, this translates into longer intervals between changeouts and lower life-cycle cost per kilogram compared to “cheaper” but short-lived options.

    Applications & Benefits

    Why this grade

    Nimonic 75 is an 80/20 nickel-chromium alloy with titanium stabilisation, and it is solid-solution strengthened only — it cannot be significantly age-hardened. That is precisely why it is chosen: it forms and welds readily with no solution-treat-and-age cycle and no strain-age cracking risk, which makes it the sheet-metal grade for gas turbine combustion hardware, furnace parts and heat-treatment fixtures.
