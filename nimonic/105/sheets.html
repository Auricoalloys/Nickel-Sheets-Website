Applications & Benefits

Why this grade

Nimonic 105 carries more cobalt and molybdenum than 90 and a higher aluminium-to-titanium ratio, raising the gamma-prime volume fraction and with it creep strength to around 950°C. It is a blade and disc alloy; the extra strength comes at the cost of fabricability, so it is not a sheet-fabrication choice.

  • Chemical Industry: High-temperature chemical reactors, catalyst supports, reformer tubes with advanced corrosion resistance.
  • Oil & Gas: Steam reformer components, high-performance heat exchangers, gas turbine engine parts requiring long service life.
  • Marine: Turbine blades, combustion chamber components with improved oxidation and thermal fatigue resistance.
  • Pharmaceutical: Furnace internals and high-purity process equipment that demand cleanliness and durability.
  • Power Generation: Gas turbine rotors, blades, and discs exposed to cyclic thermal and mechanical stresses.

FAQ & Search Trends

Nimonic 105 exhibits superior creep rupture strength and oxidation resistance at higher service temperatures (>750°C) due to higher cobalt and titanium content and optimized age-hardening.
