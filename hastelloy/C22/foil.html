Specifications and Standards for Hastelloy C22 Foil

Hastelloy C22, designated UNS N06022 and DIN/Werkstoff No. 2.4602, is a nickel-chromium-molybdenum-tungsten alloy engineered for broad-spectrum corrosion resistance. Primary specifications include ASTM B575 (sheet, plate, and strip, including foil) and ASME SB575, with EN 10088-5 (NiCr21Mo14W) for European compliance. Foil forms are typically supplied annealed for optimal formability, with thicknesses from ultra-thin gauges for liners to heavier strips for structural shims, all backed by EN 10204 3.1 mill test certificates ensuring PMI, tensile testing, and full traceability.

Industry Applications: Why Hastelloy C22 Foil Excels

Why this grade

Hastelloy C22 raises chromium to about 22% at the expense of tungsten. The extra chromium widens the margin in oxidising conditions — nitric acid, mixed acids, and oxidising chlorides such as ferric and cupric chloride — which is exactly where C276 is most likely to pit. It is the standard step up from C276 when the oxidising side of a mixed stream is the problem.

Chemical Processing

In flue gas desulfurization (FGD) scrubbers and chlorination systems, Hastelloy C22 foil resists wet chlorine, hypochlorite, and sulfuric acid at concentrations where 316L and 904L pit rapidly reducing maintenance by up to 70% in aggressive service.

Oil & Gas

Sour gas handling and deepwell sour service (NACE MR0175 compliant) favor UNS N06022 foil for its resistance to H2S, chlorides, and CO2, outperforming C-276 in oxidizing conditions.

Marine

Seawater heat exchangers and desalination plants use C22 foil for superior crevice corrosion resistance over duplex grades, especially in high-velocity, aerated flows.

Pharma

High-purity reactors and sterile processing lines specify Hastelloy C22 (FDA-compliant) to prevent contamination from corrosion products, with foil forms ideal for flexible liners and gaskets.

Power Generation

FGD systems and waste incinerators deploy C22 foil in ducts and dampers, where mixed acids and particulates accelerate failure in standard alloys studies show 3–5x longer life.
