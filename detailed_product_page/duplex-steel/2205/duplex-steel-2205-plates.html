A coastal refinery–pharma complex had built its tanks, scrubbers and seawater lines around “safe” 316L, yet within a few years chloride‑induced cracking in hot brine loops and deep pitting in caustic‑plus‑chloride wash systems forced emergency repairs, thicker replacement plates and frustrating rework in vessels that were supposed to last decades. When the asset integrity and procurement teams shifted critical shells, channel covers and seawater box plates to Duplex 2205 & 31803 Plates (UNS S32205/S31803, 1.4462), they adopted a nitrogen‑enhanced duplex stainless with about 22% chromium, 3% molybdenum and 5–6% nickel, whose typical plate data show yield strength around 450–510 MPa, tensile strength around 620–725 MPa, elongation about 25% and density near 7.8–7.9 g/cm³, with a pitting resistance equivalent number of roughly 35 and documented resistance to chloride SCC and crevice attack that far exceeds 316L; over subsequent inspections wall‑loss slowed dramatically and chloride cracking indications essentially disappeared, allowing thinner designs to outlast the original thicker austenitic plates.

Duplex 2205, commonly specified as UNS S32205 or S31803 and Werkstoff 1.4462, is a nitrogen‑enhanced duplex stainless steel consisting of roughly equal austenite and ferrite phases and containing about 21–23% chromium, 4.5–6.5% nickel, 2.5–3.5% molybdenum and 0.08–0.20% nitrogen, with the balance iron and controlled carbon, manganese, silicon, phosphorus and sulfur. Technical datasheets describe duplex 2205 as the most widely used duplex stainless grade, characterised by yield strength approximately double that of standard austenitic stainless steels, good fatigue strength, and outstanding resistance to stress‑corrosion cracking, crevice corrosion and pitting in chloride‑containing and hydrogen‑sulfide environments, useful up to about 600 °F (≈315 °C).

For procurement heads, design and corrosion engineers, fabricators, OEMs and hardware or industrial supply stores serving chemical, oil & gas, marine, pharma and power‑generation markets, Duplex 2205 & 31803 plates solve the twin challenges of strength and chloride corrosion that limit 304L/316L and many 300‑series alloys. Plate property tables for duplex 2205 show minimum tensile strength around 620–655 MPa, minimum 0.2% yield strength around 450 MPa and minimum elongation around 25%, with typical values of about 725 MPa tensile and 510 MPa yield and a density around 7.8 g/cm³ and modulus of elasticity about 200 GPa, allowing designers to down‑gauge wall thickness while maintaining or increasing allowable design stress. Corrosion literature reports that 2205’s PREN of roughly 35 and its higher critical pitting temperature place its pitting and crevice corrosion resistance significantly above 316L/317L and between that of 316L and 6% Mo super austenitics, and that its resistance to chloride stress‑corrosion cracking in hot process streams is far better than 316L, which should be avoided where chlorides and elevated temperature combine.

Applications & Benefits

  • Chemical & Oil & Gas: Duplex 2205 plates are applied in reactors, pressure vessels, process piping and heat exchangers in chemical plants and upstream oil & gas equipment where chloride‑bearing media, hydrogen sulphide and high pressure demand both high strength and resistance to SCC, pitting and crevice corrosion.
  • Marine & Desalination: In seawater cooling systems, desalination plants, ballast systems and coastal structures, UNS S32205/S31803 plates provide much higher CPT and SCC resistance than 316L and 317L, making them ideal for long‑term service in warm, aerated seawater and brine.
  • Pharma & Food: Duplex 2205 has been used in pharmaceutical processors and sanitary equipment where design codes allow higher allowable stress than 316L, enabling thinner walls and lighter equipment while still providing better pitting resistance in chloride‑containing cleaning and process solutions.
  • Power Generation: In FGD systems, condensers, feedwater heaters and coastal power‑plant piping, duplex plates deliver superior corrosion‑fatigue and erosion‑corrosion resistance compared with austenitic grades, improving reliability in high‑velocity and cyclic‑loading environments.
  • Hardware & Supply Stores: Stocking Duplex 2205 & 31803 plates alongside Nimonic 75 thin gauge sheet, Nimonic 75 shim stock and other alloys allows industrial supply stores to support customers needing both chloride‑resistant plate and high‑temperature nickel alloy sheet from one traceable source.

FAQ & Search Trends

Property comparisons show duplex 2205 has approximately double the yield strength of 316L and significantly higher fatigue strength, enabling thinner‑wall designs that still meet pressure and structural requirements. Corrosion studies report that 2205 has a higher critical pitting temperature and much better resistance to chloride stress‑corrosion cracking than 316L, which is vulnerable when chlorides and elevated temperatures combine, making duplex a safer choice for hot brine and seawater service.

Engineers typically specify duplex 2205 plates as UNS S32205 or UNS S31803, EN 1.4462 and cite ASTM A240 / ASME SA‑240 Type 2205 for plate, sheet and strip in welded pressure vessels and general service.
