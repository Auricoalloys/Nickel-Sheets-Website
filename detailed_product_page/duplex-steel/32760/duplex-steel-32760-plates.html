A North Sea gas platform had already cycled through 316L, 904L and even Duplex 2205 on its firewater ring mains, riser clamps and splash‑zone manifolds, yet every inspection still revealed new pitting around welds, crevice corrosion under supports and hairline cracks where warm natural seawater, CO₂ and vibration met. When the integrity and procurement teams rebuilt those systems in Super Duplex 32760 Plates (UNS S32760, 1.4501, F55), they chose a super duplex stainless steel with roughly 25% chromium, 7% nickel, about 3–3.6% molybdenum and 0.24–0.32% nitrogen whose PREN exceeds 40, whose typical plate data list 0.2% yield strength around 550 MPa, tensile strength around 750–800 MPa, elongation about 25% and density about 7.8 g/cm³, and whose critical pitting temperatures in chloride test solutions often reach 70–90 °C; subsequent thickness surveys showed corrosion rates in seawater falling below about 0.005 mm/year and stress corrosion cracking indications disappearing even in heated, aerated brines.

Super Duplex 32760 is a highly alloyed super duplex stainless steel that combines an approximately 50/50 austenite–ferrite microstructure with high chromium, molybdenum, nickel and nitrogen contents to deliver very high strength and outstanding resistance to pitting, crevice corrosion and chloride stress‑corrosion cracking. Datasheets describe UNS S32760 as having typical composition ranges of about 24–26% chromium, 6–8% nickel, 3–5% molybdenum, 0.24–0.32% nitrogen, with manganese normally ≤1.2%, silicon ≤0.8%, carbon ≤0.03%, phosphorus ≤0.035%, sulfur ≤0.02% and copper up to about 0.5%, with iron as the balance, and highlight PREN values above 40 as a key differentiator versus standard duplex grades.

For procurement heads, design and corrosion engineers, fabricators, OEM manufacturers and hardware or industrial supply stores serving chemical, oil & gas, marine, pharma and power‑generation sectors, Super Duplex 32760 plates address the point where 316L, 904L and Duplex 2205 cannot deliver the required safety margins in warm seawater, concentrated brines, sour gas and mixed acid–chloride service. Mechanical property tables for S32760 show a minimum 0.2% proof stress of about 550 MPa, minimum tensile strength around 750 MPa and minimum elongation around 25% for plate, with a melting point of about 1350 °C and density around 7.8 g/cm³, which together permit thinner designs at the same design pressure and provide better fatigue and erosion‑corrosion resistance than lower‑strength austenitic options. Corrosion reviews note that Duplex 32760 exhibits PREN values typically greater than 42, critical pitting temperatures over 70–80 °C in standard chloride tests and general corrosion rates in seawater below 0.005 mm/year, while SCC threshold stresses exceed yield strength in many chloride environments, effectively eliminating chloride‑induced stress‑corrosion cracking as a normal failure mode.
