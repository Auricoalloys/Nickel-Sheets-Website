A large offshore gas compression platform had already upgraded from 316L and Duplex 2205 to thicker plates in its seawater lift pumps, riser clamps and splash‑zone manifolds, but after a few years in 35 °C natural seawater the NDT reports still showed pitting around welds, crevice attack under gaskets and fatigue damage where waves and vibration combined with corrosion. When the integrity and procurement teams rebuilt those critical structures in Super Duplex 32750 Plates (UNS S32750, 1.4410), they chose a super duplex stainless steel designed for highly corrosive seawater and high‑load service, with about 24–26% chromium, 6–8% nickel, 3–5% molybdenum and roughly 0.24–0.32% nitrogen; plate data show tensile strength around 795–800 MPa minimum, 0.2% yield strength around 550–80 MPa minimum depending on specification, elongation about 15–25% and density around 7.8 g/cm³, together with excellent resistance to pitting, crevice corrosion and chloride stress‑corrosion cracking in natural seawater. Subsequent inspection campaigns reported stable wall thickness and minimal localised corrosion in areas where previous materials had failed repeatedly, even under long‑term exposure to warm, aerated seawater and cyclic mechanical loading.

Super Duplex 32750 (often referred to as Super Duplex 2507) is a super duplex stainless steel with a mixed austenitic–ferritic microstructure and high chromium, molybdenum and nitrogen contents that give it outstanding resistance to pitting, crevice corrosion and stress‑corrosion cracking in chloride‑containing environments. Typical compositional ranges reported for UNS S32750 include about 24–26% chromium, 6–8% nickel, 3–5% molybdenum and around 0.24–0.32% nitrogen with a maximum carbon content of about 0.03%, and producers highlight that this chemistry gives higher strength and better corrosion resistance than Duplex 2205 in seawater and sour‑service conditions.

For procurement heads, design and corrosion engineers, fabricators, OEM manufacturers and hardware or industrial supply stores serving chemical, oil & gas, marine, pharma and power‑generation sectors, Super Duplex 32750 plates offer a way to reduce thickness and total cost of ownership where 316L, 904L and even Duplex 2205 face unacceptable pitting, crevice attack or stress‑corrosion cracking. Representative data for ASTM A240 UNS S32750 plate list density about 7.8 g/cm³, minimum tensile strength 795–800 MPa, minimum yield strength 550–80 ksi (≈550 MPa) and minimum elongation around 15–25%, alongside Brinell hardness up to about 290–310 HBW, which gives a strength‑to‑weight ratio significantly higher than standard austenitic stainless steels and many duplex grades. Corrosion references emphasise that Super Duplex 32750 can withstand long‑term service in natural seawater with about 19 000 ppm chlorides with minimal risk of pitting or SCC, and application notes highlight excellent resistance to uniform corrosion in sulfuric and hydrochloric acids at moderate concentrations and temperatures.

Applications & Benefits

  • Chemical & Oil & Gas: Super Duplex 32750 plates are widely used in heat exchangers, pressure vessels, piping and manifolds in chemical plants and upstream oil & gas, where high Cr–Mo–N and duplex strength provide excellent resistance to pitting, crevice corrosion, SCC and erosion‑corrosion in chloride‑bearing and sour media at high pressure.
  • Marine & Desalination: UNS S32750 / 1.4410 plates are selected for seawater lift pumps, RO frames, desalination heat exchangers, coastal structures and splash‑zone components because they can withstand long‑term exposure to natural seawater with minimal risk of pitting or SCC, outperforming duplex 2205 in the harshest seawater conditions.
  • Pharma & Power: In pharma utilities, FGD absorbers, condensers and coastal power‑plant cooling systems, Super Duplex 32750 combines high strength, low thermal expansion and high thermal conductivity, which helps reduce thermal stresses and improve reliability in high‑temperature, high‑velocity and cyclic‑duty service.
