Mu metal (often marketed as HY-Mu 80 or similar trade names) is a soft ferromagnetic alloy typically containing around 80% nickel with the balance mostly iron and a few percent molybdenum, optimised for extremely high magnetic permeability and low coercive force rather than mechanical strength. Compared with stainless steels or carbon steels used as “shielding” plates, properly processed mu metal can exhibit minimum DC permeabilities of around 80,000 at modest flux densities, and maximum permeabilities reported above 100,000–300,000 in specialised grades, making it far more effective at redirecting low-frequency magnetic fields that would otherwise penetrate ordinary housings.

FAQ & Search Trends

Stainless and mild-steel plates have relatively low permeability and saturate at modest flux densities, so they only weakly divert low-frequency magnetic fields, whereas Mu Metal plates based on around 80% Ni with Fe and Mo can achieve minimum DC permeabilities on the order of 80,000 and much higher maximum values after proper annealing, making them far more effective for shielding static and power-frequency fields.

Engineers typically reference ASTM A753 Alloy 4 style specifications for high-permeability shielding alloys such as HY-Mu 80 or equivalent, using chemical composition bands around 80–82% nickel with several percent molybdenum and balance iron, and requesting data on minimum and maximum permeability, coercivity, and recommended heat treatment to ensure shielding performance.

Mu Metal plates must be carefully formed and then subjected to final annealing in a dry hydrogen atmosphere, often in a controlled magnetic field, which can increase permeability by orders of magnitude; any significant bending, machining, or mechanical shock afterward can degrade permeability and may require re-annealing to restore optimal shielding performance.
