When a new MRI suite in a Mumbai hospital began showing noisy images and false readings, engineers traced the problem to stray magnetic fields leaking from nearby power equipment and elevators threatening diagnostic accuracy and expensive downtime for procurement teams and OEMs. Switching to properly annealed Mu Metal foil shields around key electronics cut low-frequency interference dramatically, with studies showing MRI measurement errors reduced by up to 10% when high-permeability Mu Metal is correctly applied compared to basic steel enclosures .

Mu Metal (often designated HY-MU 80, Permalloy 80, or ASTM A753 Alloy 4) is a soft magnetic nickel-iron-molybdenum alloy containing about 80% Ni, ~4.2–5.2% Mo, and balance Fe, engineered for extremely high initial and maximum magnetic permeability with very low coercive force . This profile allows Mu Metal foil to absorb and redirect static and low-frequency magnetic fields that conventional stainless steels or copper cannot effectively attenuate, making it indispensable for MRI rooms, aerospace sensors, telecom racks, high-end audio transformers, and precision scientific instruments .

HY-MU 80 grades can achieve maximum permeability values greater than 350,000 when heat treated per ASTM A753, versus typical structural steels with relative permeability around 200–1,000, delivering orders-of-magnitude better shielding performance for low-flux magnetic fields . For procurement heads in chemical, oil & gas, marine, pharma, and power generation, this means more stable instrumentation, quieter signals, and fewer nuisance trips in control systems compared with cheaper “magnetic” steels that only offer partial attenuation .

FAQ & Search Trends

Standard steels and aluminum mainly block electric fields and high-frequency EMI, while Mu Metal’s very high permeability (µ up to >350,000) lets it absorb and redirect static and low-frequency magnetic fields that other metals barely affect, giving far higher attenuation for MRI, audio, and sensor systems .

Hydrogen annealing after forming can increase permeability by around 40 times, aligning grains and removing impurities that pin domain walls, while mechanical bending after annealing can reduce shielding performance until the foil is re-annealed, making quality heat treatment essential for critical MRI and aerospace installations .

Mu Metal’s permeability decreases as temperature drops, so cryogenic-grade variants or layered shielding designs are recommended for research labs; however, the alloy is widely used in space and laboratory settings for magnetic cleanliness when paired with proper design and processing .
