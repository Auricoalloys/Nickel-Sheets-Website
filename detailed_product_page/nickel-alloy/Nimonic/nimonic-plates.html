At a combined-cycle power plant, repeated creep cracking in 310S stainless hot ducting and burner tiles forced shutdowns every 9–12 months, cutting plant availability below 90% and driving maintenance budgets into the red. The turning point came when the OEM upgraded the hot gas path baffles and transition pieces to Nimonic 75 Plates (UNS N06075), a nickel–chromium superalloy that maintains strength and oxidation resistance to around 1000°C and offers creep-rupture strengths above 80 MPa at 650°C for 1000 hours, restoring availability above 98% and stretching outage intervals to more than three years.

For procurement heads, process and maintenance engineers, fabricators, OEMs, and hardware or industrial supply stores in chemical, oil & gas, marine, pharma, and power generation, Nimonic plates provide a step-change over stainless and basic nickel alloys. Nimonic 75, an approximately 80/20 nickel–chromium alloy with titanium and carbon additions, combines good corrosion and oxidation resistance with high-temperature strength and creep resistance, making it ideal for components exposed to sustained stress in hot gas paths, reformers, incinerators, and furnace roofs where 304/316/310 steels lose strength rapidly.

Applications & Benefits

  • Chemical & Oil & Gas: Nimonic plates in burner quarls, reformer furnace roofs, and hot support brackets retain strength and resist oxidation where 310S or 253MA plates creep or scale, lowering unplanned outages and inspection frequency in fired heaters and incinerators.
  • Marine & Power: In naval turbines, marine incinerators, and combined-cycle HRSG ducting, Nimonic 75’s high-temperature strength and oxidation resistance help maintain geometry and sealing surfaces under cyclic thermal loading, outperforming conventional stainless steels.
  • Pharma & High-Clean Environments: In specialty pharma and fine chemicals, Nimonic plates and thin sheet can be used in high-temperature sterilizers, thermal oxidizers, and off-gas treatment where clean, scale-resistant surfaces and mechanical stability are essential.
  • Hardware & Industrial Supply: Stocking Nimonic 75 thin gauge sheet, Nimonic 75 shim stock, and plate allows hardware and MRO distributors to respond immediately to breakdowns in turbines and furnaces, while Incoloy plate options address the associated ducting, shells, and tubesheets for a complete, specification-driven package.

FAQ & Search Trends

Nimonic 75 maintains significantly higher strength at temperatures approaching 1000°C and offers better long-term creep and oxidation resistance than 310 or 253MA, giving longer service life and allowing thinner sections in highly stressed hot components such as turbine parts and furnace tiles.

A common approach is to specify Nimonic plates and thin-gauge Nimonic sheet for the highest-stress, highest-temperature locations (burners, turbine sections, hot duct transitions), while using Incoloy 825 or 800-series plates for corrosion-resistant structural parts like vessels, tube sheets, and ducts that face acids, chlorides, or high-temperature oxidation but lower mechanical stress.

Key checks include UNS N06075 / BS HR 203 identification, EN 10204-compliant test certificates, confirmation of correct heat treatment, and mechanical properties aligned with published data (tensile around 650–800 MPa, yield around 240–275 MPa, elongation 25–40%), plus a supplier capable of cut-to-size, shim, and thin-gauge sheet supply for rapid turnaround.
