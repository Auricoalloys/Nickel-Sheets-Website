Applications & Benefits

  • Chemical & Oil & Gas: Nichrome plates and strips in immersion heaters, acid‑resistant evaporators, and tank heating coils deliver more stable heat input and longer life in oxidising or mildly corrosive atmospheres than stainless or plain FeCrAl, reducing heater changeouts and process upsets.
  • Marine & Power: In marine incinerators, auxiliary boilers, and power‑plant duct heaters, NiCr plates resist oxidation and thermal fatigue under frequent switching, improving availability compared with lower‑grade resistance materials.
  • Pharma & Food: For validation ovens, sterilisers, and dryers needing tight temperature control, Nichrome’s stable resistivity and protective chromium‑oxide scale support consistent watt density and reduce hot‑spot induced failures, which is harder to achieve with basic stainless steel strips.

FAQ & Search Trends

Nichrome plates (NiCr 80:20) offer higher electrical resistivity, stable resistance over a wide temperature range, and form a chromium‑oxide scale that protects against further oxidation at temperatures up to about 1200°C, whereas stainless steels oxidise faster, drift in resistance, and lose strength earlier in continuous high‑temperature service.

NiCr 80:20 is widely used up to about 1200°C with good oxidation resistance and stable resistivity, while NiCr 70:30 can run somewhat hotter (around 1230°C) and NiCr 60:15 or 40:20 suit moderate temperatures and specific resistance requirements; designers choose between them based on maximum element temperature, atmosphere, and targeted resistance per metre.

Key checks include the correct NiCr grade (for example NiCr 80:20 / NiCr80‑20), UNS and W.Nr. where applicable (such as UNS N06003 / 2.4869), resistivity and mechanical properties consistent with published values, and documentation referencing relevant resistance‑alloy standards like ASTM B344, along with supplier capability for plate or strip cutting and surface conditioning.
