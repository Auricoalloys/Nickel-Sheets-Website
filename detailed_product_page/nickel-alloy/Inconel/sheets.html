Inconel is a family of nickel-chromium alloys rather than a single material, and on a sheet order the grade is the decision. Aurico Alloys LLP supplies Inconel sheet in ten of them: the general-purpose corrosion grades 625 and 600, the aluminium-bearing 601, the creep-resistant 617, the low-cycle-fatigue 625 LCF, the high-molybdenum 686, the high-chromium 690, the metal-dusting-resistant 693, the age-hardened 718 and the spring grade X-750. What they share is a nickel-chromium base that holds strength and resists oxidation, pitting and stress corrosion cracking where stainless steels stop being an option – heat exchangers, pressure vessels, reactors and turbo machinery. What they do not share is a specification: each is rolled to the flat-product standard written for it, and those are listed grade by grade below.

Aurico Alloys LLP stocks Inconel sheets with thickness from 0.1 mm to 100 mm, widths up to 2500 mm, and customizable lengths, available in bright, polished, annealed, or ground finishes. With full mill test certificates including PMI and environmental compliances, our Inconel sheets meet international standards ensuring trustworthy quality for every project.

Beyond physical toughness, Inconel sheets exhibit excellent fabricability and weldability, critical for the dynamic construction of process equipment. Their stability under repeated thermal cycling and corrosive exposure reduces costly downtime and replacement cycles, offering long-term value and operational confidence.

Inconel Sheet Grades Supplied

Ten Inconel grades are supplied as plate, sheet and strip. Each has its own sheet page carrying that grade's size range, annealed properties and the specification written for it. Inconel 725, 751 and 783 are not made in flat product – their pages list the forms each one is supplied in – and for Inconel 740H, ask us which forms we can source.

Mechanical & Physical Properties

Density and melting range for all ten grades are in the Equivalent Grades & Standards tables above, each one read off the mill bulletin named beneath it.

Tensile strength, yield strength and elongation are not averaged into a single table here, because they are properties of a grade in a condition rather than of the family: an age-hardened 718 sheet and an annealed 600 sheet are not comparable numbers, and the same grade differs by thickness and temper. Each grade's own sheet page publishes its figures, and the mill test certificate supplied with your order states the results for the material you actually receive.
