FAQ & Search Trends

Unlike Invar, which targets ultra‑low expansion around 1–1.5 × 10⁻⁶ /°C mainly for precision tooling and structures, Kovar is tuned to approximately 5 × 10⁻⁶ /°C so that its CTE closely matches borosilicate glass and certain ceramics, whereas austenitic stainless steels typically sit around 16–18 × 10⁻⁶ /°C and cause high stresses at glass seals.

Kovar should be specified wherever metal components must be hermetically sealed to glass or alumina ceramics such as instrument headers, vacuum windows, and sensor packages because its expansion matches these materials and avoids cracking or leakage during thermal cycling, which stainless and carbon steels with much higher CTE cannot reliably prevent.

Engineers should request UNS K94610 / 1.3981 confirmation, compliance with ASTM F15 or equivalent controlled‑expansion specifications, certified chemical composition within narrow Ni–Co–Fe limits, mechanical properties near published values (UTS ≈ 500–520 MPa, YS ≈ 340 MPa, elongation ≥25–30%), and verified CTE curves over the intended operating range to ensure compatibility with the chosen glass or ceramic.
