A European flue-gas desulfurization (FGD) absorber handling hot, chloride-contaminated sulphuric and nitric acid mist had already chewed through rubber-lined carbon steel and then 904L and Alloy 20 plates, forcing shell repairs every 18–24 months and driving outage costs into seven figures. When the owner finally rebuilt critical zones with Alloy 59 Plates a Ni‑Cr‑Mo alloy with about 23% chromium and 16% molybdenum designed specifically for mixed oxidizing and reducing acids the corrosion rate in standard boiling acid tests dropped by an order of magnitude compared with earlier plate materials, and inspection reports showed dramatically lower pitting and weld attack after years in service.

Alloy 59 (UNS N06059, W.Nr. 2.4605) is a nickel‑chromium‑molybdenum alloy with very low carbon and silicon content, delivering high strength and outstanding resistance to pitting, crevice corrosion and stress‑corrosion cracking in both oxidizing and reducing media. Its chemistry (≈23% Cr and 15–16.5% Mo with minimal Fe) and lack of tungsten provide better thermal stability than many older Ni‑Cr‑Mo alloys, and testing shows significantly lower corrosion rates than common C276‑type alloys in aggressive sulphuric acid and mixed “green death” environments, while achieving demanding NACE MR0175 / ISO 15156 sour‑service levels used in oil & gas.

For procurement heads, process engineers, fabricators, OEMs and hardware or industrial supply stores supporting chemical, oil & gas, marine, pharma, and power plants, Alloy 59 plates offer a clear upgrade path from high‑alloy stainless steels (such as 904L) and even from versatile workhorses like C276 when maximum uptime and life in mixed acids, chlorides and FGD liquors are required. Alloy 59 plate delivers room‑temperature yield strengths above about 190 MPa, tensile strengths above roughly 540–550 MPa and elongation above 40%, retaining good impact toughness even at cryogenic temperatures around −196 °C, which makes it suitable for both cold and hot, highly corrosive duties.

Applications & Benefits

  • Chemical & Oil & Gas: Alloy 59 plates in reactors, agitator vessels, scrubbers, and column internals deliver markedly lower corrosion rates than C276 and high‑alloy stainless steels in boiling sulphuric acid and mixed acid/chloride streams, with tests showing corrosion rates around 0.625 mm/a versus 5–6 mm/a for C276 in ASTM G28A and 0.1 mm/a versus ≈1.5 mm/a in G28B, helping extend inspection intervals and reduce unplanned outages.
  • Marine & Power Generation: In FGD absorbers, stack liners, condensers, and seawater‑contaminated sections of heat exchangers, Alloy 59’s combination of high chromium and molybdenum with very low carbon and silicon provides superior pitting, crevice and SCC resistance compared with 904L and many nickel alloys, making it well suited to long‑life plate fabrications exposed to cycling temperatures and chlorides.
  • Pharma & High‑Purity Systems: For mixed acid cleaning, waste‑neutralisation and off‑gas treatment where contamination from corrosion products is unacceptable, Alloy 59 plate offers broad resistance to nitric, phosphoric, sulphuric and hydrochloric acids, including contaminated streams, while maintaining weldability with low hot‑cracking tendency, which is essential for hygienic, fully welded constructions.
