    NiCr 40:20 contains about 40% nickel and nearly 20% chromium with the balance iron, plus silicon and manganese additions. That balance is a cost-optimised resistance heating alloy: it keeps enough chromium for a protective oxide at furnace temperature while trading nickel for iron, which is what makes it the economical choice of the family. Its high electrical resistivity and form stability under cycling make it suited to heating elements, furnace fixtures, and machined resistor parts.

    Available in polished, hot-rolled, and custom cut bars with diameters ranging commonly from 6mm up to 120mm, NiCr 40:20 is stocked and supplied globally. Certified mill test reports and batch traceability provide assurance for demanding compliance requirements in pharmaceutical and power plant sectors. Hardware suppliers and OEMs value its weldability and formability across intricate fabrication applications.

    Compared to NiCr 70:30 and 80:20 alloys, NiCr 40:20 trades some element life and top service temperature for a materially lower nickel content, making it the choice where duty cycles are moderate and cost per element matters as much as peak temperature.
