FAQ & Search Trends

NiCr 40:20 provides higher resistivity, more stable resistance vs temperature, and better resistance to oxidation and chemical attack than low‑cost FeCrAl or stainless steels, giving longer life and more predictable heater performance up to 1050°C.

Compared with NiCr 80:20 and NiCr 70:30, NiCr 40:20 operates at slightly lower maximum temperature (1050°C vs 1150–1250°C) but can be more economical while still offering good oxidation resistance and mechanical stability for medium‑temperature equipment.

Nimonic 75 (UNS N06075, 2.4951) is a superalloy sheet with higher strength and better creep resistance to about 650°C and is preferred for gas turbine casings and stressed hot parts, whereas NiCr 40:20 is optimized for electrical resistance heating rather than high mechanical stress.
