Across chemical, oil & gas, marine, pharma, and power generation, procurement heads now specify NiCr 80:20 for load banks, industrial kilns and offshore immersion heaters where generic stainless or low-nickel plates simply cannot survive .

NiCr 80:20 often called Nichrome 80/20 is a high-resistance 80% nickel, 20% chromium alloy engineered for continuous service up to about 1200°C, combining stable electrical resistivity with excellent oxidation resistance even under frequent thermal cycling . Its electrical resistivity is around 1.08–1.12 Ω·mm²/m at 20°C, significantly higher than iron–nickel–chromium alternatives, which allows designers to use shorter, more compact elements or thinner plates to achieve the same power rating . For OEM manufacturers and hardware or industrial supply stores, that means fewer hot spots, more predictable ohmic values, and lower warranty claims compared to low-cost resistance alloys .

Mechanically, Alloy 80/20 maintains yield strength of at least 280 MPa and tensile strength of at least 650 MPa at room temperature, with elongation of about 30%, giving far better formability and bend life than brittle FeCrAl at equivalent temperatures . This combination of ductility and high creep resistance in oxidizing atmospheres is why NiCr 80:20 is widely used in industrial furnace heating elements, electric kilns, high‑power resistor banks, and chemical incinerators worldwide . For marine and offshore users, its resistance to hot corrosion in oxidizing gas mixtures like air, nitrogen and combustion products makes it a safer bet than basic stainless for immersion heaters and subsea flowline tracing .
