Frequently Asked Questions

What distinguishes Nichrome 80:20 from 60:15 foil?

Nichrome 80:20 (UNS N06023, DIN 2.4869) offers highest oxidation resistance to 1200°C with 1.08 μΩ·m resistivity for precision heating. 60:15 grade balances cost with 1150°C service via Fe addition. Both meet ASTM B344; 80:20 preferred for vacuum furnaces.

Are Nichrome foils weldable for element fabrication?

Yes, all grades TIG weld directly without filler using DCEN. 70:30 grade minimizes hot cracking in thick sections. Post-weld anneal optional at 1050°C. Fabricators achieve 100% joint strength per AWS D17.1 aerospace quals.

What foil thickness for rapid heating elements?

0.025-0.08 mm foils optimize 30-second heat-up for lab/pharma ovens. Thinner 0.01 mm for ultra-fast thermocouples. All oxide-coated per ASTM B810 for uniform emissivity. Complements Titanium Grade 5 UNS R56400 ASTM B265 in hybrid designs.

How to select Nichrome grade by application?

80:20 for max temperature/oxidation; 60:15 for cost-effective industrial; 40:20 for sulfidation resistance. Verify resistivity stability via ASTM B344 tests. Procurement specs include grain size ASTM E112 for consistent performance.
