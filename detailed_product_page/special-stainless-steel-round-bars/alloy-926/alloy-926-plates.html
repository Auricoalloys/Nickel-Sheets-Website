A phosphoric‑acid and fertiliser complex on a tidal coast had already cycled through 316L, 904L and rubber‑lined carbon steel in its contaminated sulfuric–phosphoric–chloride loops, yet each shutdown the inspectors mapped new pits around nozzles, weld overlays and tube sheets, and the finance team watched unplanned plate replacements eat into margins. When the reliability and procurement teams rebuilt the most abused reactor heads, flash cooler plates and seawater‑exposed manifolds in Alloy 926 Plates (UNS N08926, 1.4529), they upgraded to a 6‑moly super austenitic alloy developed as an enhanced version of 904L, with roughly 24–26% nickel, 19–21% chromium, about 6.5% molybdenum, copper and around 0.2% nitrogen; published data show tensile strength in the 650–850 MPa range, yield strength above 300 MPa, elongation above 40% and density about 8.1 g/cm³, together with a pitting resistance equivalent number around 47, and over several campaigns thickness readings in those circuits stabilised where previous plates had repeatedly thinned and cracked.

Alloy 926 (also marketed as Incoloy 926, Cronifer 1925 hMo and UR 926) is a super‑austenitic stainless steel containing about 6–7% molybdenum and an addition of nitrogen, described as an enhanced version of Alloy 904L with significantly improved resistance to pitting and crevice corrosion in chloride‑containing environments. The nickel and chromium contents provide resistance to a wide range of corrosive environments, particularly non‑oxidising acids such as sulfuric and phosphoric, while the high molybdenum and nitrogen give excellent resistance to localised corrosion, and copper further improves sulfuric‑acid resistance.

For procurement heads, design and corrosion engineers, fabricators, OEM manufacturers and hardware or industrial supply stores serving chemical, oil & gas, marine, pharma and power‑generation assets, Alloy 926 plates offer a way to move up from 316L, 904L and many duplex grades when chloride levels, acid strength or temperature push those alloys to their limits but full nickel‑base alloys are hard to justify. Sources emphasise that Alloy 926 shows excellent resistance to pitting and crevice corrosion in halide media and sulfur‑containing hydrogen environments, very good resistance to chloride‑induced stress‑corrosion cracking and excellent general corrosion resistance in both oxidising and reducing media, including diluted sulfuric and phosphoric acids even when contaminated with chlorides. Mechanical data indicate tensile strengths around 690 MPa, yield strengths around 330 MPa and elongation about 42% in 2 in, which are higher than those of 316L and 904L and allow engineers to use thinner sections for a given design load.

Applications & Benefits

  • Chemical & Oil & Gas: Alloy 926 plates are used in sulfuric‑ and phosphoric‑acid production equipment, salt plant evaporators, pollution‑control systems and acid gas handling, where high Mo and N deliver excellent resistance to localised attack and very good SCC resistance in chloride‑containing and H₂S‑containing environments.
  • Marine & Power: In sea‑ and brackish‑water cooler shells, service water piping, feedwater heaters and offshore platform equipment, UNS N08926 / 1.4529 plates provide high resistance to pitting, crevice corrosion and SCC at temperatures up to about 400 °C in both oxidising and reducing media.
  • Pharma & Food: Alloy 926 is applied in food and chemical processing, pulp‑mill bleaching and high‑purity plants where halides, mixed acids and high temperatures coexist but contamination limits and weldability requirements rule out many nickel‑base alloys.
