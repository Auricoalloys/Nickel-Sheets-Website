A Middle Eastern desalination plant feeding coastal power generation had already upgraded seawater intake screens and tube sheets from 316L to duplex stainless, but within 18 months the inspection divers reported the familiar sight of pitting clusters around welds and gasket faces where biofouling and stagnant chloride pockets had concentrated. When the engineering team redesigned the most critical condenser plates, strainer baskets and pipe spools in SMO 254 Plates (UNS S31254, EN 1.4547), they switched to a super austenitic alloy with 19.5–20.5% chromium, 17.5–18.5% nickel, 6–6.5% molybdenum and PREN values typically above 40, delivering yield strength around 310 MPa, tensile strength around 650 MPa and elongation above 35% at a density of about 7.98 g/cm³; subsequent inspections showed clean, pit-free surfaces where previous materials had failed, extending maintenance intervals from 18 months to over 4 years.

SMO 254 (also known as 254 SMO, 6Mo, or 20Mo) is a high-performance super austenitic stainless steel containing approximately 20% chromium, 18% nickel and 6% molybdenum, with controlled nitrogen additions for enhanced pitting resistance. Designated UNS S31254 and EN 1.4547, plate and sheet products are typically supplied under ASTM B625 / ASME SB-625 specifications, with mechanical properties including minimum tensile strength of 650 MPa, minimum yield strength of 310 MPa and minimum elongation of 35% in the solution-annealed condition. The alloy achieves a pitting resistance equivalent number (PREN) typically ranging from 40–43, positioning it between conventional austenitic grades and nickel-base alloys in chloride resistance.

For procurement heads, corrosion engineers, fabricators, OEMs and hardware supply stores serving chemical, oil & gas, marine, pharma and power-generation sectors, SMO 254 plates fill the critical gap where 316L/317L suffer rapid pitting and crevice corrosion, but fully nickel-base alloys prove cost-prohibitive. Industry corrosion data demonstrate that SMO 254 maintains corrosion rates below 0.1 mm/year in seawater at temperatures up to 40°C, resists chloride stress-corrosion cracking up to 80°C where 316L fails above 60°C, and provides superior resistance to dilute sulfuric and phosphoric acids compared to standard austenitics.
