A sour‑gas gathering station on a coastal cluster had already replaced carbon steel spools with 316L and 904L in its wet H₂S and chlorides‑laden manifolds, yet every turnaround the UT readings told the same story: grooving around welds, pitting in dead legs and worryingly fast wall‑loss in the phosphoric‑acid injection loops. When the integrity team redesigned the most punished header plates, spool sections and absorber internals in Alloy 28 Plates (UNS N08028, WNR 1.4563), they switched to a nickel–iron–chromium alloy with around 30–32% Ni, 26–28% Cr, 3–4% Mo and 0.6–1.4% Cu which data show has excellent resistance to both oxidising and reducing acids, stress‑corrosion cracking and localised attack in sulphuric, phosphoric and chloride‑bearing service, and inspection reports over several campaigns confirmed that in areas where 316L and even 904L had struggled, the new plates remained essentially unaffected.

Alloy 28 (also marketed as Sanicro 28 and Incoloy 28) is an austenitic nickel–iron–chromium alloy with additions of molybdenum and copper, developed specifically for aggressive wet‑process phosphoric acid and other strong acid environments. The alloy is designated UNS N08028 and WNR 1.4563, and plate and sheet are commonly supplied under standards such as ASTM B709 for Alloy 28 plate and related specifications for wrought corrosion‑resistant alloys. Published data show tensile strengths typically around 500–750 MPa with yield strengths from about 220–250 MPa and elongation around 35–45% depending on product form, with Brinell hardness roughly 180 HB and a pitting resistance equivalent number (PREN) around 39, indicating strong resistance to chloride pitting and crevice corrosion.

For procurement heads, corrosion and mechanical engineers, fabricators, OEMs and hardware or industrial supply stores serving chemical, oil & gas, marine, pharma and power‑generation projects, Alloy 28 plates occupy a strategic space: more corrosion‑resistant than 316L and 904L in many contaminated acid and chloride environments, yet often more cost‑effective than fully nickel‑base alloys. Studies and datasheets highlight that Alloy 28 has very good resistance to sulphuric and phosphoric acid, including chloride‑contaminated solutions, plus excellent resistance to stress‑corrosion cracking in chloride‑bearing and H₂S‑containing conditions, making it attractive for phosphoric acid units, sour‑gas systems, FGD equipment, seawater‑cooled exchangers and nuclear fuel or pollution‑control services.
