Applications & Benefits

Why this grade

Haynes® 230 is a nickel-chromium-tungsten-molybdenum alloy with a lanthanum addition. It is the all-round choice in the range, combining creep strength and oxidation resistance to about 1150°C with the thermal stability and fabricability that older alloys such as Hastelloy X do not have, plus useful resistance to nitriding atmospheres.

  • Chemical & Petrochemical: Catalyst grid supports in ammonia/nitric acid burners, high-temperature heat exchangers, ducts, bellows, and process internals where oxidation and nitridation are severe.
  • Oil & Gas & Power: Combustion cans, transition ducts, flame holders, thermocouple sheaths, and hot-gas manifolds in gas turbines, plus superheater and reheater tubing in advanced ultra-supercritical boilers.
  • Marine: High-temperature exhaust components and furnace fixtures that need better oxidation resistance than basic Ni–Cr alloys.
  • Pharma: Clean, scale-resistant furnace baskets and fixtures supporting validated heat-treatment cycles.
  • Industrial Heating: Furnace retorts, chains, fixtures, burner shrouds, recuperator internals, nitriding furnace internals, grates, trays, sparger tubes, cyclone internals, and thermocouple protection tubes.
  • Hardware & OEM Stores: Stock Haynes 230 sheet metal for jet engine components and high-temperature fasteners to offer a premium upgrade over standard stainless and Nimonic 81 in static hot-zone parts.

FAQ & Search Trends

Haynes 230 combines higher rupture strength, better oxidation and nitridation resistance up to about 1150°C, and improved structural stability compared with iron–nickel–chromium and many Nimonic grades, leading to significantly longer service life and fewer forced shutdowns.

Technical datasheets describe Haynes 230 as readily formable and weldable using standard nickel-alloy procedures, with good resistance to microstructural degradation in service so that components can be repaired without complete re-solution treatment in many cases.
