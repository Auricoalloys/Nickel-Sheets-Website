Applications & Superiority vs Nimonic 75

Why this grade

Haynes® 25, also known as L-605, is a cobalt-nickel-chromium-tungsten alloy with high strength to about 980°C. What separates it from the nickel-base grades is wear and galling resistance, so it is specified for turbine vanes and combustor parts that also see rubbing contact, for bearings and bushings, and for surgical implants.

  • Chemical: Sulfidation-resistant furnace muffles, carburizing baskets – superior to nickel alloys
  • Oil & Gas: Gas turbine nozzle guide vanes, combustor liners enduring thermal fatigue
  • Marine: Afterburner components, bearing sleeves with galling resistance
  • Pharma: High-temperature kiln trays maintaining clean surfaces
  • Power: Industrial turbine shrouds, flame holders to 980°C
  • Hardware: Nimonic 75 thin gauge sheet complement for hybrid designs
