Inconel is the nickel-chromium core of the superalloy range, and the widest family we stock. The useful first cut is between the solid-solution grades and the age-hardened ones. Solid-solution grades take their strength from what is dissolved in the matrix, stay easy to weld and form, and are chosen for corrosion or oxidation resistance. Age-hardened grades precipitate a strengthening phase during heat treatment, which buys a great deal of strength at temperature and costs fabricability.

Within that, 625 and 600 are the general-purpose corrosion and heat grades, 718 is the age-hardened workhorse for aerospace and downhole hardware, and the rest are specialists chosen for a particular environment or temperature.

Inconel Alloys

Inconel Alloys for Critical Applications

Inconel alloys are a family of austenitic nickel-chromium-based superalloys that are known for their outstanding resistance to oxidation and corrosion at high temperatures. These materials are primarily used in applications requiring high strength and oxidation resistance in environments that exceed 1,000°F (537°C).

Key Properties:

  • High Temperature Strength
  • Corrosion and Oxidation Resistance
  • Excellent Weldability
  • Thermal Fatigue Resistance
  • Resistance to Scaling and Sulfidation

Applications:

  • Aerospace and Aviation
  • Power Generation
  • Chemical Processing
  • Marine and Offshore

Where These Alloys Are Used

  • Aerospace — turbine casings, rings, fasteners and exhaust components
  • Oil and gas — downhole tools, wellhead hardware and sour service tubulars
  • Chemical processing — reactors, heat exchangers and acid service piping
  • Marine — seawater systems and splash zone hardware, generally in 625
  • Power generation — superheaters, reheaters and steam generator tubing
  • Additive manufacturing — gas atomised powder in 718 and 625
