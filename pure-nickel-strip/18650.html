    What is an 18650 Nickel Busbar?

    An 18650 cell is 18 mm across and 65 mm long, and on its own it is worth very little to a pack builder. Cells are wired into parallel groups and those groups into series, and the part that does the wiring is a nickel busbar — a strip of pure nickel cut so that one piece welds to every cell in a group at once. It carries the group's whole current, so its cross-section sets how hard the pack can be worked and how warm it gets doing so.

    The material matters more than the shape here. Pure Nickel 200 has a resistivity of about 8.5 µΩ·cm against nickel-plated steel's 20+ µΩ·cm, so a pure strip wastes less of the pack's energy as heat in the interconnect, and its ductility keeps the joints intact under the vibration that cracks plated strip. What the geometry then decides is how that material is distributed across the cell group — and that is what separates the three types below.

    The Three 18650 Busbar Types

    All three are cut from the same Nickel 200 / 201 strip and all three weld the same way. They differ in how the metal is laid out over the cell group, which is a question about your layout rather than about the material. Follow the link for the sizes, contact distances and full specification of each.

    Type What it is Typical use Stocked sizes
    H-Type Contact pads bridged into an H, so current is distributed evenly across the pair and heat is carried away from the weld. The default for 2P / 3P / 4P groups on a regular pitch, hand-cut or machine-stamped. 0.15 / 0.20 mm thick; 26, 27, 45, 47 and 72.8 mm wide; contact distance 19.0, 20.2, 33.24 and 34.5 mm.
    Honeycomb A honeycomb pattern through the strip, which raises the surface available for heat to leave and gives a consistent weld footprint over each cap. Densely packed groups where the interconnect has to shed heat as well as carry current. 0.15 – 0.20 mm thick; 6 – 25 mm wide; rolls typically 1 m or 5 m.
    Zig Zag An interlocking current path down the strip, which holds voltage drop down and keeps weld performance stable along a long run. Large-format and high-current builds, where a straight strip would develop hotspots. 0.15 – 0.20 mm thick; 6 – 25 mm wide; cut to length, rolls up to 5 m.

    Choosing a Type for Your Pack

    Four questions settle it in most cases, and the answers come off your own layout rather than from us.

    • What is the cell pitch? The contact distance of an H-type part has to match the centre spacing of the cells it welds to. That is why the H-type page lists contact distances alongside widths — pick the CD first and the width follows.
    • How many cells in parallel? A 2P group and a 4P group want different widths of the same material, because the strip carries the whole group's current. Going wider is usually cheaper than going thicker, since thicker strip is harder to weld.
    • How hard is the pack worked? Continuous high discharge is where honeycomb and zig zag earn their shape — one for shedding heat, the other for holding voltage drop down over a long run. A light-duty pack rarely needs either.
    • How is it being welded? Pure nickel resists resistance spot welding precisely because it conducts so well, and the difficulty rises with thickness. If you are at the limit of your welder, that is a case for laser welding rather than for dropping to plated steel.

    If the layout does not match anything stocked, send the drawing — the part is cut from the same strip either way.

    Other Cell Formats & Related Products

    The same three geometries are stocked for every cylindrical format we supply. If your pack uses a larger cell, start here.

    21700 Nickel Strips & Busbars

    The format most new EV and e-mobility designs move to from 18650. Higher capacity per cell means fewer, larger parallel groups — and a busbar carrying more current per link.

    26650 Nickel Strips & Busbars

    Wider cells on a coarser pitch, common in industrial and backup packs. Contact distances and strip widths are correspondingly larger than the 18650 parts.

    32650 / 32700 Nickel Strips & Busbars

    Large-format cylindrical cells for solar, backup and electric transport, in H-type, honeycomb and zig zag with contact distances to suit.

    Nickel-Copper Sandwich Busbar

    Where nickel alone cannot carry the current: a copper core for conductivity with nickel faces that still spot weld to the cell. Also see copper busbar.
