Alloy 31 (UNS N08031)

Alloy 31 (UNS N08031, W.Nr. 1.4562) carries about 27% chromium, 31% nickel, 6.5% molybdenum and a nitrogen addition — a combination that gives it exceptional resistance to hot sulphuric and phosphoric acid as well as to chloride pitting. It is used in acid plant, flue-gas cleaning, seawater systems and pickling lines where lower-alloyed grades corrode away.

How Alloy 31 Compares

Alloy UNS / EN How it differs
Alloy 31 (Nicrofer 3127 hMo) N08031 / 1.4562 6–7% molybdenum with 0.15–0.25% nitrogen on a 26–28% chromium base — the most pitting- and crevice-resistant of the four, and the one specified for hot sulphuric and phosphoric acid.
Alloy 28 (Sanicro 28) N08028 / 1.4563 Similar chromium and nickel but 3.5% molybdenum against Alloy 31’s 6–7%. Long-established in phosphoric acid and sour-gas service, and the cheaper choice where that lower molybdenum is enough.
904L N08904 / 1.4539 The 4% molybdenum grade a step below both. Good in dilute sulphuric acid; outmatched by either alloy above in chloride-bearing media.
316L S31603 / 1.4404 Conventional austenitic stainless, listed for context. General-purpose corrosion resistance, and the grade these alloys are specified in place of when it pits.

Molybdenum, chromium and nitrogen figures for Alloy 31 and Alloy 28 are from the mill data cited in the table above. Grade selection depends on the specific medium, temperature and concentration — talk to us before specifying.
