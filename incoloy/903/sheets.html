Why this grade

Incoloy 903 is a controlled-expansion superalloy: a nickel-cobalt-iron base with essentially no chromium, giving a low and nearly constant coefficient of thermal expansion together with a constant modulus to about 650°C. It is used where running clearance has to be held — gas turbine rings, casings and shrouds. Carrying no chromium, it has no inherent oxidation resistance and is normally coated.

  • Incoloy 903 for gas turbine casings: Compressor casings, turbine shrouds requiring constant CTE matching rotor materials
  • High-strength alloy for steam turbine bolts: Large diameter bolting maintaining preload through thermal cycling
  • Incoloy 903 rocket engine thrust chambers: Cryogenic to combustion temperature components with dimensional stability
  • Incoloy 903 for ordnance hardware: Precision guidance components surviving launch shock and thermal extremes
  • Chemical Process: High-pressure reactor vessels, thermal cycling components
  • Oil & Gas: Subsea valve actuators, high-pressure manifolds

FAQ Incoloy 903 vs 909 Age Hardening

Solution anneal 980°C/1h + water quench, then age 650°C/8h furnace cool. Achieves 1725 MPa UTS, 1450 MPa 0.2%YS, 15% elongation.

Both have constant CTE 7.2 µm/m/°C to 650°C, but 903 offers higher strength (1725 MPa vs 1380 MPa UTS) at lower cost. 909 used where ultra-low expansion to 730°C required.
