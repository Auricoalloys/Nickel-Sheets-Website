Why Incoloy 800 Sheets Are Essential for Harsh Conditions

Incoloy 800 (UNS N08800, ASTM B409) stands out for its impressive resistance to oxidation and carburization at elevated temperatures up to 1100°C, alongside reliable corrosion resistance in reducing and moderately oxidizing environments. This makes it a preferred alloy in steam generators, heat exchangers, and reactors operating in power plants and chemical facilities.

The alloy’s chemistry, incorporating 30–35% nickel, 19–23% chromium, and small additions of iron, ensures a balanced matrix that resists detrimental phase changes and mechanical embrittlement. Its excellent thermal stability and strength retention at high temperatures support long service life under cyclic and severe operating conditions.

Applications & Benefits

Why this grade

Incoloy 800 is a 32% nickel, 21% chromium alloy on an iron balance. It is the general-purpose member of the family for heat and corrosion resistance up to about 650°C, and the least expensive of the 800 series. Above that temperature its creep strength falls away and 800H or 800HT applies instead.

  • Furnace parts exposed to high-temperature oxidation and carburization
  • Chemical process equipment including reactors and heat exchangers
  • Marine propulsion and seawater handling systems
  • Power generation heat exchangers, superheaters, and boilers
  • Pharmaceutical and sterilization equipment requiring corrosion resistance
