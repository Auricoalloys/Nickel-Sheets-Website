When a large waste‑to‑energy plant in Europe pushed its boiler outlet above 1100°C to burn lower‑grade fuels, conventional stainless and standard Incoloy 800H/800HT tube sheets began to warp, oxidize, and crack in less than a year, forcing shutdowns that cost millions in lost power revenue and penalties. This is exactly the scenario facing chemical, oil & gas, marine, pharma, and power generation facilities that are raising firing temperatures, changing fuels, or extending run lengths: the traditional heat‑resistant alloys that worked at 850–950°C no longer deliver safe margins at 1050–1150°C, and each unexpected tube sheet failure or furnace panel distortion becomes a direct hit to EBITDA.

Incoloy 890 was engineered as a nickel‑iron‑chromium alloy with about 43% Ni and 25% Cr plus additions of molybdenum, niobium, and silicon to offer high strength along with excellent resistance to oxidation, carburization, and sulfidation up to about 1200°C, outperforming classic 800H/800HT in creep and stress‑rupture while maintaining good fabricability for plates, sheets, and tubes.

Applications & Industry Benefits

  • Chemical & Petrochemical: Radiant/convection section plates, tube sheets, and baffles in high‑temperature heaters, crackers, and reformers where carburization and sulfidation from process gas or fuel cause rapid failure of 800H/800HT or stainless alloys.

FAQ & Search Trends

Published data show that Incoloy 890 has tensile and yield strengths roughly comparable to alloy 803 and slightly higher than 800HT, with creep and stress‑rupture resistance essentially matching 803 and exceeding 800HT in the upper temperature range, due to its higher chromium and additions of molybdenum and niobium.

High‑temperature stainless grades usually lose creep strength and suffer severe oxidation, carburization, and sulfidation well below 1200°C, whereas Incoloy 890 is specifically designed for these aggressive conditions and maintains superior scale integrity and mechanical properties for much longer service life.
