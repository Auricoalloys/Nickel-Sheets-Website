Waterjet Cutting

Transform Your Industrial Cutting with Precision Waterjet Technology

Waterjet cutting systems operate by propelling a high-pressure jet of water, often mixed with abrasive garnet particles, at velocities exceeding 600 meters per second to precisely cut through materials. The typical maximum thickness capability ranges up to 150mm depending on material type. This cold-cutting method maintains the original mechanical properties without causing thermal deformation.

Waterjet Cutting Specifications & Industry Standards

Waterjet cutting systems operate by propelling a high-pressure jet of water, often mixed with abrasive garnet particles, at velocities exceeding 600 meters per second to precisely cut through materials. The typical maximum thickness capability ranges up to 150mm depending on material type. This cold-cutting method maintains the original mechanical properties without causing thermal deformation.

Suppliers adhering to UNS (Unified Numbering System) materials, such as UNS S31803 for duplex stainless steel or UNS N06625 for Inconel alloys, ensure product traceability and industry compliance. ASTM standards such as ASTM A276 for stainless steel bars and shapes are critical in verifying the chemical and mechanical properties of the materials pre and post cutting, offering assurance for critical applications.

Product Grades & Chemical Properties

Waterjet cutting is compatible with a wide range of product grades used in industries like chemical processing and power generation. For example, duplex stainless steels such as UNS S31803 and S32205 are prized for their corrosion resistance and strength, typically containing 22% chromium, 5-6% nickel, and 3% molybdenum. Chemical specs meet ASTM A789 and A182 standards for seamless and welded tubular products.

Mechanical properties include tensile strengths upwards of 75 ksi with excellent yield strength and elongation, ensuring parts endure extreme environmental stress. Waterjet cutting does not alter these properties due to its non-thermal nature, unlike plasma or laser cutting.

Common Uses Across Industries

  • Chemical industry: fabrication of corrosion-resistant piping, valves, and reactors.
  • Oil & Gas: precise cutting for offshore platforms, pipelines, and refinery components.
  • Marine: manufacturing shipbuilding components and seawater-resistant assemblies.
  • Pharmaceutical: sterile, contamination-free parts for medical equipment and instrumentation.
  • Power Generation: turbine components and heat exchangers requiring metallurgical integrity.
