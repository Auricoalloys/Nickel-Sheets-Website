Laser Welding for Battery Tabs and Busbars

Joining Pure Nickel Without Heating the Cell

A lithium cell is a sealed chemical system with a temperature limit, and every tab joined to it is a chance to exceed that limit. Laser welding puts the energy where the joint is and nowhere else: the pulse lasts milliseconds, the fused zone is a fraction of a millimetre across, and the can below it never reaches a temperature that damages the jelly roll or the seal. That is the whole reason pack builders reach for it — not speed, but the ability to make a sound joint in pure nickel at full thickness while leaving the cell alone.

Why pure nickel resists the spot welder

Resistance spot welding works by putting a current through a joint that resists it. Pure Nickel 200 has a resistivity of about 8.5 µΩ·cm against nickel-plated steel's 20+ µΩ·cm — the property that makes it the better conductor in a finished pack is exactly the property that makes it hard to spot weld. The current takes the easy path, the heat appears in the cell rather than at the interface, and the answer on a spot welder is more energy, which is the last thing a cell should be given. Builders who work around it by dropping to thinner strip or to plated steel give up the low resistance they were buying nickel for. A laser does not depend on the workpiece resisting anything, so pure nickel strip can be joined at the thickness the pack actually needs.

What we weld

  • Tab to cell: pure nickel tabs onto the caps and cans of cylindrical cells — 18650, 21700, 26650 and 32650 / 32700.
  • Strip to busbar: series and parallel links between H-type, honeycomb and zig zag busbars and the strip that carries the current between groups.
  • Dissimilar joints: nickel to copper, including tabs onto nickel-copper sandwich busbar and copper busbar, where the nickel face is what gets welded.
  • Fusing links: the narrow sections cut into a busbar to act as a fuse, welded without annealing the very feature they were cut for.

Materials and certification

We weld the material we stock: Nickel 200 and Nickel 201 to ASTM B162 (UNS N02200 / N02201), copper busbar, and nickel-clad copper. Because the strip and the weld come from the same place, the EN 10204 3.1 mill test certificate for the material follows the job through processing rather than stopping at the despatch of a coil — the pack builder gets one traceable chain, not two.

Thickness, joint configuration, cell format and pitch are quoted against your pack drawing at the time of enquiry. We would rather answer for your layout than publish a range that turns out not to cover it.

Laser welding against resistance spot welding

Both have their place, and most packs use both. The table is the short version of when the laser earns its cost.

ConsiderationLaser weldingResistance spot welding
Heat reaching the cellConfined to a fused zone a fraction of a millimetre wideCurrent passes through the cell itself, so heat spreads
Pure nickel at full thicknessUnaffected by the material's low resistanceHarder as thickness and purity rise
AccessSingle-sided; the beam needs line of sight onlyNeeds electrode access to both sides of the joint
Nickel to copperPractical, with the joint designed for itDifficult — copper conducts the current away
ConsumablesNone; nothing wears against the workElectrode tips dress and wear, and drift with them
Best suited toThicker pure nickel, dissimilar metals, tight or one-sided geometryThin plated strip in open, repetitive layouts
