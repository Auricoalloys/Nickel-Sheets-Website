Sheet or Plate? The Thickness Boundary

The difference is defined by thickness, not by use, and the dividing line is the same 4.75 mm (0.1875 in, or 3/16 in) across the material standards this site quotes. Below it the mill calls the product sheet; at or above it, plate. Enquiries that ask for "3 mm plate" or "8 mm sheet" get re-quoted against the correct standard, which costs a day, so it is worth naming the thickness rather than the word.

FormThicknessTypically supplied as
FoilUnder 0.15 mmSlit coil or cut lengths
Strip0.15 mm to 4.75 mm, under 600 mm wideCoil, slit to width
SheetUnder 4.75 mm, 600 mm wide and overCut lengths from coil, or discrete sheets
Plate4.75 mm and overDiscrete plate, cut to size

Width matters as well as thickness. The same 2 mm material is strip below 600 mm wide and sheet at 600 mm and over, and it is ordered against different tolerance tables in each case.

Surface Finish and Edge Condition

Finish is set by how the sheet leaves the mill, and it is not cosmetic. It governs how the surface behaves in service, how well it passivates, and how it takes a weld or a polish afterwards.

FinishRouteWhere it is specified
No. 1Hot rolled, annealed, descaledHeavier gauges, structural and general fabrication where the surface is not critical
2DCold rolled, annealed, pickledDull matt surface, good for deep drawing and where a coating follows
2BCold rolled, annealed, pickled, skin passedThe default for process plant: smoother than 2D, passivates well
BABright annealed in a controlled atmosphereReflective, no pickling scale; pharmaceutical and high-purity service
No. 4Mechanically polishedVisible architectural and food-contact surfaces

Edges are supplied as mill edge or slit edge as standard. Sheared, laser-cut and waterjet-cut edges are available; waterjet avoids the heat-affected zone that matters on the corrosion-resistant grades, which is why it is usually the right choice on Hastelloy® and the super austenitics.

Where two families both work, the decision is usually fabrication rather than corrosion: how the sheet welds, whether it needs post-weld heat treatment, and whether the grade is available in the thickness you want without a mill run.

What to Include in a Sheet Enquiry

Quoting is faster when the enquiry carries these six things. The first three fix the price; the last three fix whether the material is usable when it arrives.

  • Grade and specification — for example Inconel® 625 to ASTM B443, or duplex 2205 to ASTM A240. The specification matters as much as the grade.
  • Thickness, width and length — in millimetres, with the tolerance standard if your drawing calls one up.
  • Quantity — in pieces or kilograms. Sheet is priced per kilogram and cut from stock, so a part sheet is quoted differently from a full one.
  • Finish and edge — 2B, BA, No. 1 or polished; mill edge or cut.
  • Certification — EN 10204 3.1 is supplied as standard. Specify 3.2 if a third-party inspector must countersign, and name the inspection body.
  • Any supplementary testing — intergranular corrosion testing to ASTM A262 or G28, ferrite count on duplex, ultrasonic testing, or a positive material identification report.

Material is sheared, laser or waterjet cut, slit or sawn to size before despatch, and every consignment carries an EN 10204 3.1 mill test certificate. More on quality and certification, or send us an enquiry.
