How We Verify Material Before It Ships

Every consignment is checked against its mill certificate on arrival, not on despatch. That means reading the markings rolled or stamped into the material, confirming the alloy with a handheld analyser, and photographing both before the material is cut. Mill Markings and Traceability

Material arrives marked by the producing mill with the specification, the dimensions and a heat number. That heat number is what ties the plate in front of you to the EN 10204 3.1 certificate issued with it — if the two do not agree, the material does not enter our stock.

Positive Material Identification

Incoming nickel alloy is checked with a handheld X-ray fluorescence analyser before it is released to stock. The readings below were taken on two Alloy 600 plates from heat 31001 and are reproduced exactly as the instrument reported them, including its stated uncertainty.

What this test does and does not show. Handheld XRF confirms the grade and the major alloying elements. It cannot read carbon, sulphur, boron or the other light elements, so it complements the mill’s wet-chemistry analysis rather than replacing it. Every order ships with the mill’s EN 10204 3.1 certificate; the PMI check is how we satisfy ourselves that the certificate belongs to the metal.

Export Packing

Material is wrapped, edge-protected and crated in ISPM 15 treated timber before it leaves us, with the packing list fixed to the case and heat numbers on every label.

Case Study — Alloy 602 CA Against a 20-Day Deadline

A client came to us with an urgent requirement for Alloy 602 CA and twenty days to work with. We sourced 8 mm VDM Alloy 602 CA (2.4633 / UNS N06025) in the hot-rolled, annealed and scaled condition, brought it in by air cargo against the mill’s EN 10204 3.1 certificate, cut it to the sizes required and delivered complete in sixteen days — four days inside the deadline.
