In a petrochemical refinery, a combustion liner made from conventional nickel alloy suffered rapid oxidation and creep deformation at 1100°C, causing unplanned downtime and repair costs exceeding $500K.[web:54][web:52] This scenario illustrates the vital role of Haynes alloy wire, particularly Haynes 230 (UNS N06230), in withstanding extreme heat and corrosive gases. Procurement professionals and OEM engineers specify it for reliable performance in power generation, chemical processing, and oil & gas.

Haynes 230 wire complies with UNS N06230, ASTM B622 (seamless pipe/tube), B619 (welded pipe), DIN 17744 No. 2.4733, and EN NiCr22W14Mo. These standards guarantee oxidation resistance up to 1149°C and suitability for nitriding atmospheres.

Additional specs like AMS 5878 support aerospace-derived industrial uses.

Equivalent Grades

Equivalents include Haynes 625 (UNS N06625), Haynes 188 (UNS R30188), Haynes 25 (UNS R30605, DIN 2.4835), with similar compositions for cross-material substitution. Haynes 282 (UNS N07208) offers age-hardening variants.

Product Forms and Availability

Offered in fine wire (0.03-0.125 inches), coils, straight, welding filler (e.g., 230-W ERNiCrWMo-1 AWS A5.14), and shaped forms for springs, meshes. Diameters up to 0.156 inches suit fabricators; complement with Haynes alloy hex bar for fixtures.
