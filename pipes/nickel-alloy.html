In a North Sea offshore platform, carbon steel pipes transporting sour gas corroded catastrophically within months, leading to leaks, shutdowns, and $10M+ in remediation. This failure exemplifies why nickel alloy pipes, such as Inconel 625 (UNS N06625) and Monel 400 (UNS N04400), are indispensable for harsh environments. Fabricators, OEMs, and procurement heads select them to safeguard operations in oil & gas, chemical processing, and marine sectors.

Specifications and Standards

Nickel alloy pipes adhere to ASTM B622 (seamless), B619 (welded), B167 (Inconel seamless), DIN/EN 17751/17752, and ISO 15156 for sour service. Grades like UNS N06625 (Inconel 625), N04400 (Monel 400), N10276 (Hastelloy C276) ensure compliance.

NACE MR0175/ISO 15156 certifies H2S resistance for oil & gas.

Frequently Asked Questions

What standards govern nickel alloy pipes?

ASTM B622 seamless, B619 welded, B167 Inconel; DIN/EN 17751, NACE MR0175. Cover UNS N06625, N04400 etc., ensuring corrosion/pressure ratings for oil & gas, chemical use.

What properties make nickel alloy pipes superior?

UTS 500-1000 MPa, yield 200-800 MPa, 30-50% elongation, high melt point 1350°C+. Resist creep, acids, extremes; ideal high-pressure corrosive flows.

Which industries use nickel alloy pipes?

Chemical (reactors), oil & gas (subsea), marine (seawater), power/pharma (boilers). Outperform SS in H2S/acids; vital offshore/power growth.

What sizes are nickel alloy pipes available in?

1/8"-24" OD, SCH 10-160/XXS, seamless/welded, lengths to 12m. Custom per API/ASME for OEMs.
