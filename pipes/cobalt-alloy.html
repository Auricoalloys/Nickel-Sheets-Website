At a heavy-duty gas turbine repair facility in the UAE, Haynes 25 (UNS R30003) transition duct piping failed prematurely after 18,000 hours due to thermal fatigue and sulfidation attack from fuel impurities. The cracking at weld joints, combined with wall thinning from hot gas erosion, forced an unscheduled major overhaul costing $3.4M and 45 days of downtime.

Post-failure analysis revealed that nickel-based alternatives could not maintain structural integrity under the combined stresses of 1050°C cyclic operation and abrasive particulates. The upgraded solution—Cobalt Alloy XB seamless pipes—delivered 2.5x the expected service life, proving why cobalt alloys remain the gold standard for the most punishing high-temperature industrial applications.

Specifications and Standards

Cobalt alloy pipes conform to stringent international specifications ensuring reliability in extreme conditions.

  • ASTM/ASME Standards:
    ASTM B622/ASME SB572: Seamless and welded Co-Cr-Ni-Mo alloy pipe (XB).
    ASTM B707/ASME SB707: Co-Cr-W-Ni alloy pipe (Haynes 25/L-605).
    ASTM F75: Cast Co-Cr-Mo alloy (basis for wrought products).
  • UNS Designations:
    UNS R30003 (XB/L-605), R30016 (188), R30015 (MP35N).
  • DIN/EN Standards:
    DIN 2.4778 (Haynes 25), 2.4851 (188).

These standards specify tolerances down to ±0.05mm for OD/wall thickness, critical for precision OEM turbine and valve applications.

Chemical Composition

UNS R30003 (Haynes 25): Co (Balance ~51%), Cr 19-21%, W 14-16%, Ni 9-11%, Fe ≤3%, Mn ≤1.5%, Si ≤0.4%, C 0.05-0.15%.

The high Tungsten content provides solid-solution strengthening, while Chromium ensures oxide scale stability. Carbon forms fine M₇C₃ carbides for wear resistance.

FAQ

UNS R30003 vs R30016?

R30003 (Haynes 25) has 15%W for superior hot strength; R30016 (188) has 22%Ni, La for weldability. Both excel at 1050°C+.
