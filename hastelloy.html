Hastelloy is a family of nickel-molybdenum and nickel-chromium-molybdenum alloys built for wet corrosion — the cases where stainless steel pits, cracks or dissolves. Choosing between them comes down to one question about the process stream: is it reducing or oxidising?

The B grades are nickel-molybdenum and excel in reducing acids, hydrochloric above all, but they have little chromium and so fare badly if the stream turns oxidising. The C grades add chromium to the molybdenum and handle both, which is why they are the usual answer when a plant sees mixed or variable conditions. The remaining grades are specialists: G30 for phosphoric acid, N for molten fluoride salts, and X for strength and oxidation resistance at temperature rather than wet corrosion at all.

Hastelloy Alloys for Critical Applications

Hastelloy alloys are a family of austenitic nickel-chromium-based superalloys that are known for their outstanding resistance to oxidation and corrosion at high temperatures. These materials are primarily used in applications requiring high strength and oxidation resistance in environments that exceed 1,000°F (537°C).

Key Properties:

  • High Temperature Strength
  • Corrosion and Oxidation Resistance
  • Excellent Weldability
  • Thermal Fatigue Resistance
  • Resistance to Scaling and Sulfidation

Where These Alloys Are Used

  • Chemical processing — reactors, heat exchangers, columns and piping in acid service
  • Pollution control — flue gas desulphurisation, scrubbers and ducting

  • Pharmaceutical — vessels and agitators where product purity rules out iron pickup
  • Oil and gas — sour service components and downhole tooling
  • Gas turbines — combustion cans and transition ducts, in Hastelloy X
