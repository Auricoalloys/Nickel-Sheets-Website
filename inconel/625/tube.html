Aurico Alloys LLP supplies Inconel 625 tube (N06625, Werkstoff 2.4856) to ASTM B444 from Mumbai, to customers across India and in more than 40 export markets.

Inconel 625 tube goes where chlorides would pit a stainless tube through. Seawater-cooled heat exchangers, offshore hydraulic and chemical-injection lines, and small-bore instrument tubing on a platform all run in the environment 625 was designed for: its 8–10% molybdenum and 3.15–4.15% niobium resist the pitting and crevice corrosion that attack 316L tube in warm seawater.

Mechanical and Physical Properties

Annealed 625 tube runs around 830–965 MPa tensile with yield above 415 MPa – roughly twice the yield of 316L, which is what lets a high-pressure control line or an exchanger tube run a thinner wall. It is strengthened without heat treatment, so bends and tube-to-tubesheet welds keep their properties as made.

Industry Applications

Why Inconel 625 tube

Two properties put 625 in tube form. Its pitting and crevice resistance survive seawater and chloride brines at the temperatures heat exchangers run at, and its strength in the annealed condition lets the wall come down, which matters in long offshore lines where every kilo is lifted. It is the default for chloride service; where hot, contaminated acid has already defeated 625, step up to 686 tube.
