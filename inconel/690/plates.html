When a nuclear and thermal power operator reviewed its fleet, the most worrisome findings were not in the turbines but inside aging steam generator tubing and high-temperature heater shells, where legacy alloys had started showing stress corrosion cracking and oxide spallation after decades in high‑temperature, high‑pressure, mildly alkaline water containing chlorides and oxygen. A shift to Inconel 690 Plates (UNS N06690, W.Nr. 2.4642) – a high‑chromium nickel alloy originally developed to overcome Alloy 600 SCC issues – brought a chemistry with roughly 58–63% nickel and about 27–31% chromium that achieves excellent resistance to oxidizing aqueous environments, low corrosion rates in many acids (for example, around 0.8 mm/year in 20% phosphoric acid at 60°C and below 0.8 mm/year to 85% acid at 80°C) and markedly better steam‑generator stress corrosion cracking performance than Alloy 600, allowing later generations of nuclear steam generators and power‑plant components to meet more demanding design lives.

INCONEL alloy 690 (UNS N06690 / W.Nr. 2.4642) is described as a high‑chromium nickel alloy with excellent resistance to many corrosive aqueous media and high‑temperature atmospheres, particularly oxidizing conditions. The substantial chromium content (about 30%) gives it outstanding resistance to oxidizing chemicals and high‑temperature oxidizing gases, while the high nickel content imparts resistance to stress‑corrosion cracking in chloride‑containing environments and sodium hydroxide solutions, making it attractive for nuclear steam generator tubing, pickling systems and nitric or nitric/hydrofluoric acid services.
