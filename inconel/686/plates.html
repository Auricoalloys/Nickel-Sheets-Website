Corrosion audit data showed wall‑losses approaching 0.5–1.0 mm/year in some sections and repeated leaks at weld heat‑affected zones, but when the most critical linings, tower sections and condenser plates were rebuilt in Inconel 686 Plates (UNS N06686, W.Nr. 2.4606), a single‑phase Ni‑Cr‑Mo‑W alloy described as having “outstanding resistance to general corrosion, stress‑corrosion cracking, and pitting and crevice corrosion” with PREN values around 51, corrosion rates in 80% sulfuric acid at 80°C of about 0.1 mm/year and Critical Pitting and Crevice Temperatures above 85°C in ASTM G48 ferric chloride testing, inspection intervals extended and unplanned leaks dropped to essentially zero in those upgraded circuits.

INCONEL alloy 686 (UNS N06686 / W.Nr. 2.4606) is a single‑phase, austenitic nickel‑chromium‑molybdenum‑tungsten alloy developed specifically to resist both strongly reducing and strongly oxidising mixed acid environments, especially those containing high halide levels. Its high nickel and molybdenum contents provide resistance in reducing media, high chromium content provides resistance to oxidising media and the combined molybdenum and tungsten additions give exceptional resistance to localised attack such as pitting and crevice corrosion, while very low iron and carbon contents and carefully controlled microstructure minimise intergranular precipitation and preserve corrosion resistance in weld heat‑affected zones.
