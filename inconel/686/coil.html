Aurico Alloys LLP supplies Inconel 686 coil, sheet and slit strip (N06686, Werkstoff 2.4606) to ASTM B575 and B906 from Mumbai, to customers across India and in more than 40 export markets.

Much of the 686 sold in flat form ends up as a lining. Thin sheet welded over carbon-steel flue-gas ducts, stack flues and scrubber shells puts 686’s corrosion resistance where the acid condensate forms, without paying for a solid 686 structure. Coil is the economical way to buy it for that work, because the lining is laid in long runs of consistent gauge.

Mechanical and Physical Properties

Sheet and strip are supplied solution annealed, at around 720 MPa tensile, with the ductility to form and fit a lining. Carbon at 0.01% maximum keeps the many seam welds of a lining free of carbide sensitisation.

Industry Applications

Why Inconel 686 coil

A flue-gas lining sees the worst of both chemistries – condensing acid with chlorides and fluorides, cycling between wet and dry as the unit loads and unloads. 686 resists that in oxidising and reducing conditions alike, which is why it is chosen where earlier linings have failed. Lining with thin sheet rather than building in solid alloy is what makes it affordable at duct and stack scale.
