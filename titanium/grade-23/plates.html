A multinational pharma and specialty‑chemicals site on the coast had already upgraded a high‑value hydrogen peroxide preparation skid from 316L to standard Ti‑6Al‑4V plates, but repeated start‑stop cycles and vibration around a high‑speed pump caused fatigue cracking in several brackets and a nozzle cover, raising uncomfortable questions about fracture margins in such a critical unit. When the mechanical integrity team re‑engineered these fracture‑critical plates and supports in Titanium Grade 23 Plates (Ti‑6Al‑4V ELI, UNS R56407), going from a conventional Ti‑6Al‑4V chemistry to extra‑low‑interstitial material with yield strength typically in the 120–160 ksi (≈828–1100 MPa) range and improved fracture toughness, inspection reports over subsequent campaigns showed clean surfaces, stable bolt loads and no indications in areas that had previously cracked, even though the density remained around 0.160 lb/in³ (≈4.43 g/cm³), less than 60% of steel.

Titanium Grade 23 is the extra‑low‑interstitial (ELI) version of Ti‑6Al‑4V, containing about 6% aluminium and 4% vanadium with significantly reduced levels of oxygen, nitrogen and other interstitial elements compared with standard Grade 5. The alloy is designated UNS R56407 and commonly associated with material numbers such as 3.7165, and plate, sheet and strip products for industrial and medical use are supplied under specifications including ASTM B265 Grade 23, AMS 4907 and implant‑focused standards such as ASTM F136. Typical data from producers show density around 0.160 lb/in³ (≈4.43 g/cm³), yield strength ranges around 120–160 ksi (≈828–1100 MPa), tensile strength ranges around 125–174 ksi (≈862–1200 MPa) and elongation ranges of roughly 10–18% depending on product and orientation.

For procurement heads, corrosion and mechanical engineers, fabricators, OEMs and hardware or industrial supply stores serving chemical, oil & gas, marine, pharma and power‑generation projects, Titanium Grade 23 plates bring the already high strength and corrosion resistance of Ti‑6Al‑4V together with improved fracture toughness and fatigue performance due to their lower impurity levels. Sources describe Grade 23 as a high‑toughness, high‑fatigue‑strength alpha‑beta alloy used in fracture‑critical biomedical and aerospace components, with data showing high‑cycle fatigue life improvements and strong performance in corrosive media such as physiological solutions and chloride environments when properly processed.

Titanium Grade 23 uses the same basic Ti‑6Al‑4V chemistry as Grade 5 but with extra‑low interstitial levels, which producers describe as improving fracture toughness, crack‑growth resistance and high‑cycle fatigue strength compared with standard Ti‑6Al‑4V while maintaining similar strength levels.
