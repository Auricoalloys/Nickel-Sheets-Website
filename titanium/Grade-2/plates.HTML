On a refinery’s seawater-cooled condenser line, 316L tube sheets and channel covers were lasting barely five years before crevice corrosion and MIC forced costly weld repairs and unplanned outages. After the plant upgraded those critical components to Titanium Grade 2 Plates (UNS R50400), inspection reports began showing essentially no measurable seawater-side corrosion, even though titanium plate weighs about 40% less than steel thanks to its 4.5 g/cm³ density, and the condenser has now run reliably through multiple turnarounds with only routine cleaning instead of heavy repair work.

Titanium Grade 2 is a commercially pure (CP) titanium with slightly higher oxygen and iron than Grade 1, giving it higher strength while retaining excellent ductility, weldability and corrosion resistance in oxidising, neutral and mildly reducing environments. It carries UNS designation R50400 and material number 3.7035, and is the most widely used CP titanium grade for industrial service because it balances a minimum yield strength of about 275 MPa (40 ksi) with outstanding resistance to seawater, moist chlorides, many acids, alkaline solutions and hot industrial gases.

For procurement heads, process and corrosion engineers, fabricators, OEMs and hardware or industrial supply stores in chemical, oil & gas, marine, pharma and power generation, Titanium Grade 2 plates provide a compelling alternative to 316L, 904L and even some duplex and nickel alloys wherever weight reduction, long life in seawater or chemicals, and compliance with NACE and pressure vessel codes are priorities. CP Grade 2 titanium’s strongly adherent oxide film gives it excellent resistance to general, pitting and crevice corrosion in natural and industrial waters, often surpassing stainless steels in such environments while operating continuously up to around 425–540 °C depending on service conditions.

Applications & Benefits

  • Chemical & Oil & Gas: Titanium Grade 2 plates are used in heat exchangers, reactors, storage tanks and piping where oxidising acids, moist chlorides, chlorite/hypochlorite solutions, nitric and chromic acids, and many organic acids would attack stainless steels, with the protective titanium oxide film resisting both general and localised corrosion.
  • Marine & Power Generation: In seawater-cooled condensers, desalination plants, offshore platforms and cooling-water systems, Grade 2’s excellent resistance to seawater and marine atmospheric corrosion combined with very good resistance to erosion and cavitation when properly designed gives far longer life than 316L or even super austenitic grades.
  • Pharma & High-Purity Systems: Titanium Grade 2 plate is used for clean-in-place systems, waste treatment, and equipment where low metal ion release and smooth, corrosion-resistant surfaces reduce contamination risk, often outperforming stainless steels in oxidising media used in cleaning and sterilisation.

FAQ & Search Trends

Titanium Grade 2 forms a strongly adherent, self-healing oxide film that provides excellent resistance to seawater, marine atmospheres, moist chlorides and many oxidising and mildly reducing chemicals, where stainless steels can suffer pitting, crevice corrosion and stress-corrosion cracking. The combination of low density, good ductility and long-term corrosion resistance often gives lower lifecycle cost, especially in critical condensers, heat exchangers and seawater systems where stainless replacements are frequent.

Engineers normally specify Titanium Grade 2 as UNS R50400 (3.7035) and call up ASTM B265 (or ASME SB-265) Grade 2 for plate, sheet and strip, while other forms use related standards such as ASTM B348 for bar and B338/B861 for tubes. Including these designations in RFQs ensures that suppliers meet the correct chemistry, mechanical properties and testing requirements for critical chemical, oil & gas, marine, pharma and power applications.

Titanium Grade 2 offers higher strength than Grade 1 while retaining excellent formability and weldability, making it the preferred CP grade for most industrial plate applications where moderate strength and high corrosion resistance are needed. Compared with Ti‑6Al‑4V, Grade 2 is easier to fabricate, weld and qualify for pressure equipment, and its corrosion resistance in seawater and many chemicals is at least comparable or better, so high-strength alloys are reserved for heavily loaded components while Grade 2 handles most plate duties.
