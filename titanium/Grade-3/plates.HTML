A coastal chemicals complex had already tried 316L, duplex stainless and even Grade 2 titanium tube sheets in a high-velocity seawater/bleach cooling loop, yet erosion corrosion and occasional pitting at the inlets kept forcing patch repairs and unplanned downtime. When the reliability team upgraded critical tube sheets and channel covers to Titanium Grade 3 Plates (UNS R50550, 3.7055), they gained roughly 20–30% higher yield strength than comparable Grade 2 plate while retaining excellent seawater and oxidising-chemical resistance, and subsequent inspections showed no significant wall loss in areas where stainless and even softer titanium had previously struggled.

Titanium Grade 3 is a commercially pure (CP) titanium with higher oxygen content than Grades 1 and 2, delivering higher mechanical strength while maintaining good ductility, weldability and corrosion resistance in highly oxidising to mildly reducing environments. It is designated UNS R50550 with material number 3.7055 and is covered for sheet and plate by ASTM B265 and ASME SB‑265, which specify composition limits on elements such as nitrogen (≤0.05%), carbon (≤0.08%), oxygen (≤0.35%), iron (≤0.30%) and hydrogen (≤0.015%) to protect its mechanical and corrosion behaviour for pressure and structural service.

For procurement heads, engineers, fabricators, OEMs and hardware or industrial supply stores serving chemical, oil & gas, marine, pharma and power-generation projects, Titanium Grade 3 plates offer a distinct step up from Grade 2 when higher allowable design stresses are needed without moving to expensive alloyed titanium grades. CP Grade 3 combines a density around 4.51 g/cm³ (less than 60% that of steel) with typical tensile strengths reported near 585–595 MPa and yield strengths around 345–462 MPa in plate, while retaining elongation of about 18–25%, giving an excellent strength-to-weight ratio and good fabrication response.

  • Chemical & Oil & Gas: Titanium Grade 3 plates are used in heat exchangers, reactors, piping and vessel components where both higher plate strength and excellent corrosion resistance are needed in oxidising acids, chlorides, bleaching solutions and other aggressive media, with performance underpinned by the alloy’s passive oxide layer.

FAQ & Search Trends

Titanium Grade 3 offers higher yield and tensile strength than Grade 2 while maintaining similar ductility, formability and corrosion resistance, giving designers higher allowable stresses and thinner, lighter plate sections in pressure and structural components. Compared with stainless steels, Grade 3’s passive titanium oxide film provides superior resistance to seawater, many oxidising chemicals and localised corrosion forms such as pitting and crevice attack, which reduces unplanned maintenance in critical condensers, heat exchangers and process equipment.

Engineers normally specify Titanium Grade 3 as UNS R50550 with material number 3.7055 and cite ASTM B265 (or ASME SB‑265) Grade 3 for strip, sheet and plate, while implant and bar products may also refer to ASTM F67 and aerospace documents like AMS 4900 and AMS‑T‑9046 CP2. Using these standards in RFQs and purchase orders helps ensure that plate meets the correct chemistry limits, mechanical properties and test requirements for regulated chemical, oil & gas, marine, pharma and power applications.

Titanium Grade 3 is preferred when plate components require higher strength and ASME allowable stresses than Grades 1 or 2 can provide, but the design still benefits from the excellent corrosion resistance and weldability of commercially pure titanium. This makes Grade 3 attractive for high-load structural components, tube sheets, channel covers and chemical containers where corrosion resistance is critical and design stresses or impact loads exceed what Grade 2 is normally chosen to carry.
