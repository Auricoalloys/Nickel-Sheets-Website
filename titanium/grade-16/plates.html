Titanium Grade 16 is an unalloyed, commercially pure titanium modified with a small palladium addition typically about 0.04 - 0.08% by weight designated Ti‑0.05Pd and listed under UNS R52402. It is often described as a “lean palladium” version of the more heavily alloyed Ti‑0.15Pd Grade 7, offering similar corrosion‑resistance mechanisms at lower noble‑metal content and thus lower cost, while retaining the good toughness, weldability and formability of CP titanium with standard oxygen levels.

For procurement heads, corrosion and mechanical engineers, fabricators, OEMs and hardware or industrial supply stores in the chemical, oil & gas, marine, pharma and power sectors, Titanium Grade 16 plates provide a step up from Grade 2 when crevice corrosion, hot brines or mixed oxidising/reducing acids are a concern, but budgets cannot justify full Grade 7 or exotic nickel alloys. Material data indicate that Grade 16 sits between Grade 7 and Grade 2 in strength and corrosion performance, with tensile strength and ductility similar to CP titanium and palladium‑enhanced resistance in halide and acid systems that extend safe operating windows.

Grade 16 keeps the weldability, toughness and density of CP titanium but the small palladium addition improves resistance in chlorides and mildly reduces acids, positioning it between Grade 7 and Grade 2 on corrosion performance at a leaner alloy cost than full‑palladium grades. Stainless steels can suffer pitting and crevice corrosion in hot chloride and acid service, whereas palladium‑modified titanium alloys maintain very low corrosion rates and stable passive films in many such environments when properly designed and fabricated.

Engineers normally specify Titanium Grade 16 as Ti‑0.05Pd or Ti‑0.04Pd, UNS R52402 and call up ASTM B265 / ASME SB‑265 Grade 16 for strip, sheet and plate, where Grade 16 is defined as unalloyed titanium plus 0.04–0.08% palladium and Grade 16H as the higher‑strength variant with at least 400 MPa UTS. Including UNS, ASTM and, where relevant, EN or material‑number references in RFQs and purchase orders helps procurement teams ensure that plates delivered for chemical, oil & gas, marine, pharma and power applications match the intended corrosion and mechanical performance.
