A coastal power station had already upgraded its seawater intake and condenser tube sheets from 316L to Titanium Grade 2, yet high-velocity brine with entrained sand still caused worrying erosion corrosion at the inlets and weld transitions over successive shutdowns. When the asset team rebuilt the most highly stressed tube sheets and baffle plates in Titanium Grade 4 Plates (UNS R50700, 3.7065), they moved to the strongest of the commercially pure titanium grades minimum yield strength about 480 MPa, tensile strength around 550–580 MPa while keeping the same excellent resistance to seawater, brine and oxidising media, and inspection records over the next cycles showed stable wall thickness where stainless and even softer CP titanium had previously thinned.

Titanium Grade 4 is a commercially pure titanium with the highest oxygen and iron levels permitted among the CP grades, which increases its strength compared with Grades 1–3 while maintaining very good ductility, weldability and corrosion resistance. It is identified as UNS R50700 and material number 3.7065, and plate and sheet are typically specified to ASTM B265 / ASME SB‑265 Grade 4 and ASTM F67 for unalloyed titanium, with chemistry limits such as oxygen up to about 0.40%, iron up to 0.50%, nitrogen up to 0.05%, carbon up to 0.08% and hydrogen up to 0.015% by weight.

For procurement heads, corrosion and mechanical engineers, fabricators, OEMs and hardware or industrial supply stores in chemical, oil & gas, marine, pharma and power-generation sectors, Titanium Grade 4 plates provide a way to push design stresses higher than Grade 2 or 3 while still benefitting from CP titanium’s low density (less than 60% of steel) and superior resistance to brine, salt solutions, oxidising and alkaline media, organic acids and seawater. Typical data sheets quote minimum tensile strengths around 550–552 MPa, minimum yield strengths around 480 MPa and elongation at break of roughly 15–20%, giving a strength-to-weight ratio comparable to many stainless steels but with far better corrosion and corrosion-fatigue performance in seawater.

Applications & Benefits

  • Chemical & Oil & Gas: Titanium Grade 4 plates are used in heat exchangers, reactors, pressure vessels, piping components and well equipment where high strength and very good resistance to oxidising acids, chlorides, brine and corrosive waste streams are needed, with corrosion-fatigue resistance that allows thinner sections than many steels.
  • Marine & Power Generation: In seawater-cooled condensers, desalination plants, offshore structures and high-load marine machinery, Grade 4’s combination of strength comparable to many stainless steels and superior resistance to seawater and chlorine-containing media provides long life and structural weight reduction.
  • Pharma & High-Purity Systems: Titanium Grade 4 plates support equipment where high mechanical strength, corrosion resistance and cleanliness are needed such as corrosive waste treatment vessels, heat exchangers and structural supports around high-purity circuits without the ion-release issues associated with corroding steel surfaces.

FAQ & Search Trends

Titanium Grade 4 provides the highest yield and tensile strength of all commercially pure titanium grades while maintaining good ductility, weldability and formability, which allows designers to use thinner plate sections and higher allowable stresses than Grade 2 or 3 in many pressure and structural applications. Compared with stainless steels, Grade 4 combines similar or better strength with significantly lower density and much better resistance to seawater, brine, oxidising and alkaline media and corrosion fatigue, leading to longer life and fewer unplanned outages in critical condensers, heat exchangers and chemical equipment.

Engineering and procurement documents typically specify Titanium Grade 4 as UNS R50700 and material number 3.7065, and call up ASTM B265 (or ASME SB‑265) Grade 4 for strip, sheet and plate, with ASTM F67 used for unalloyed titanium in some applications. Aerospace and specialised projects may also cite AMS 4901 or AMS‑T‑9046 CP1, and referencing these standards ensures that suppliers meet the correct composition, mechanical properties and test requirements for chemical, oil & gas, marine, pharma and power service.

Titanium Grade 4 is especially attractive where both high strength and excellent corrosion and corrosion-fatigue resistance are needed such as seawater-cooled condensers, high-load marine components, high-pressure chemical vessels and structural parts in strongly oxidising or chloride environments without moving to more expensive alloyed titanium grades. In many of these duties, Grade 2 may not provide enough design margin and Grade 3 may still require thicker sections, whereas Grade 4’s higher strength allows designers to optimise wall thickness and weight while preserving the corrosion performance that makes commercially pure titanium so valuable.
