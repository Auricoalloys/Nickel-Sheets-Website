A coastal power plant had already upgraded condenser tube sheets to commercially pure titanium to combat seawater corrosion, but the high‑temperature exhaust ducting on a nearby chemical waste‑heat boiler still relied on heavy 310 stainless plates that warped and cracked after repeated thermal cycles. When the engineering team re‑designed the hottest duct sections and support plates in Titanium Grade 6 Plates (Ti‑5Al‑2.5Sn, UNS R54520), finite‑element checks showed weight reductions of more than 40% versus stainless alongside creep resistance up to roughly 425 °C and oxidation resistance reported up to about 480 °C, and inspection data over successive outages confirmed dimensional stability and clean surfaces where the old steel plates had buckled and scaled.

Titanium Grade 6 is the alpha‑titanium alloy Ti‑5Al‑2.5Sn, containing around 5% aluminium and 2–3% tin with the balance titanium, designed to offer medium strength, very good weldability and fabricability, and excellent microstructural stability at elevated temperatures. The alloy carries UNS designation R54520 with EN or DIN numbers such as 3.7115, and product forms including strip, sheet and plate are commonly produced to specifications such as ASTM B265 Grade 6 and AMS 4910 or 4918 for aerospace and industrial service. Typical data show tensile strengths reported between roughly 820–900 MPa, yield strengths near 750–827 MPa and elongation close to 15–18%, providing a useful combination of strength and ductility for plate components in hot, corrosive environments.

For procurement heads, corrosion and mechanical engineers, fabricators, OEMs and hardware or industrial supply stores serving chemical, oil & gas, marine, pharma and power‑generation projects, Titanium Grade 6 plates occupy a valuable middle ground between commercially pure titanium (Grades 2–4) and high‑strength Ti‑6Al‑4V Grade 5. Grade 6’s alpha microstructure and composition give it excellent thermal stability and oxidation resistance, with sources indicating service temperatures up to about 480 °C, creep resistance to around 800 °F (≈425 °C) and good corrosion resistance in seawater and many chemical environments, especially where both elevated temperature and mechanical loads must be carried.

FAQ & Search Trends

Titanium Grade 6 combines medium tensile and yield strengths in the 800 900 MPa and 750 MPa ranges with high‑temperature stability up to roughly 480 °C and creep resistance around 800 °F, while stainless steels often suffer scaling and reduced strength in the same temperature windows. Compared with CP titanium, Grade 6 offers higher strength and improved hot‑load capability, and compared with Grade 5 it retains better thermal stability in some elevated‑temperature applications, making it attractive for hot corrosive areas where both mechanical and corrosion performance are critical.

Engineers normally specify Titanium Grade 6 as Ti‑5Al‑2.5Sn, UNS R54520 with EN or DIN number 3.7115 and call up ASTM B265 / ASME SB‑265 Grade 6 for strip, sheet and plate, with aerospace documents such as AMS 4910, AMS 4918 and related forging and bar standards used for other product forms. Including these designations in RFQs and purchase orders helps procurement heads and EPCs compare compliant offers and ensure traceable plate supply for regulated chemical, oil & gas, marine, pharma and power applications.

In many plants, Titanium Grade 6 plates are used for hot but not extreme‑temperature components such as exhaust ducting, hot seawater boxes and structural panels where temperatures up to roughly 425 480 °C and corrosive service require both strength and corrosion resistance, while Nimonic 75 plate and thin‑gauge sheet carry the highest‑temperature furnace, burner and turbine duties. Designing and sourcing both materials together allows procurement heads, OEMs and hardware or industrial supply stores to optimize cost and performance by using Ti‑5Al‑2.5Sn where its alpha‑alloy stability excels and Nimonic 75 where nickel‑based alloys are indispensable.
