A coastal chemical plant running hot hydrochloric and sulphuric acid scrubbers had already upgraded from 316L to commercially pure titanium, yet tight flange gaps and gasket interfaces in the absorber still showed crevice attack after each shutdown. When the integrity team rebuilt the critical liner, baffles and flange rings in Titanium Grade 7 Plates (Ti‑0.15Pd, UNS R52400), exposure tests showed that the small palladium addition drove crevice and pitting resistance far beyond non‑alloyed titanium in chloride‑rich reducing acids, and plant inspections over multiple campaigns reported bright, intact titanium where the previous plates had suffered measurable metal loss.

Titanium Grade 7 is an unalloyed titanium modified with a small palladium addition of roughly 0.12–0.25% by weight (Ti‑0.15Pd), giving it mechanical and physical properties very similar to Grade 2 but with much higher resistance to reducing acids and localised attack in hot halide media. The alloy carries UNS R52400 and material number 3.7235, and products such as strip, sheet and plate are covered by ASTM B265 / ASME SB‑265 Grade 7, with Grade 7H defined in the same standard as a higher‑strength variant requiring a minimum ultimate tensile strength of about 400 MPa (58 ksi).

For procurement heads, process and corrosion engineers, fabricators, OEMs and hardware or industrial supply stores in chemical, oil & gas, marine, pharma and power generation, Titanium Grade 7 plates offer a way to unlock titanium’s seawater and oxidising‑media resistance while also addressing crevice corrosion and reducing‑acid exposure that challenge plain CP titanium and stainless steels. Laboratory work and industry guides show that Ti‑0.15Pd alloys outperform unalloyed grades in hydrochloric and sulphuric acids and resist crevice corrosion at temperatures and chloride levels where Grade 2 alone starts to suffer, while maintaining a density near 4.51 g/cm³ and yield strengths in the 275–345 MPa range typical of CP titanium plate.

FAQ & Search Trends

Titanium Grade 7 retains the strength and ductility of Grade 2 but the small palladium addition significantly improves resistance to reducing acids and to crevice and pitting corrosion in chloride‑rich environments where Grade 2 and stainless steels can fail. Test data and field experience show Ti‑0.15Pd outperforming non‑alloyed titanium in hot HCl and H₂SO₄ and maintaining seawater resistance up to elevated temperatures, making it especially attractive for scrubbers, absorbers and tight‑crevice geometries in chemical and oil & gas plants.

Engineers typically specify Titanium Grade 7 as Ti‑0.15Pd, UNS R52400 with material number 3.7235 and cite ASTM B265 / ASME SB‑265 Grade 7 for strip, sheet and plate, while Grade 7H in the same standard provides a higher minimum tensile strength level. Using these designations in RFQs and purchase orders ensures that suppliers match the correct palladium content, mechanical properties and test requirements for critical chemical, oil & gas, marine, pharma and power applications.

In many facilities, Titanium Grade 7 plates are used wherever aggressive wet chemistry, reducing acids and chlorides threaten equipment life, while Nimonic 75 sheet cut to size, Nimonic 75 thin gauge sheet and Nimonic 75 shim stock provide structural integrity in very high‑temperature furnaces, burners and turbine sections. Designing with both materials allows procurement heads and OEMs to allocate Ti‑0.15Pd to the most corrosive aqueous environments and reserve nickel‑based Nimonic 75 for the hottest locations, optimising lifecycle cost and reliability across the plant.
