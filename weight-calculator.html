How the calculation works

Every figure on this page is volume multiplied by density. The volume comes from the dimensions you type, the density from the material you pick, and the two are combined once:

weight (kg) = volume (mm³) × density (g/cm³) ÷ 1,000,000

The divisor is two conversions rolled into one — cubic millimetres to cubic centimetres, then grams to kilograms. These are the volume formulas behind each product form:

Volume of one piece, by product form. B is flange width, H overall depth, tf flange thickness and tw web thickness.
Product form Volume of one piece
Sheet / Plate length × width × thickness
Coil / Strip (by length) width × thickness × length
Coil (by coil OD and ID) π/4 × (OD² − ID²) × strip width
Flat Bar width × thickness × length
Round Bar / Wire π/4 × diameter² × length
Square Bar side² × length
Hexagonal Bar 0.8660 × (across flats)² × length
Pipe / Round Tube (OD × wall) π × wall × (OD − wall) × length
Round Tube / Hollow Bar (OD × ID) π/4 × (OD² − ID²) × length
Square Tube [side² − (side − 2t)²] × length
Rectangular Tube [width × height − (width − 2t)(height − 2t)] × length
Angle (leg A + leg B − t) × t × length
T Bar [B × tf + (H − tf) × tw] × length
Channel and Beam [2 × B × tf + (H − 2tf) × tw] × length
Circle / Disc π/4 × diameter² × thickness
Ring / Flange Blank π/4 × (OD² − ID²) × thickness

Channel and beam share one formula because the two ways of measuring them are the same expression. A beam is two full-width flanges with a web between them, 2Btf + (H − 2tf)tw. A channel is a full-depth web with two flanges standing off it, 2(B − tw)tf + Htw. Expand the second and it becomes the first.

Rolled sections read light, and by how much

The four structural forms — angle, tee, channel and beam — are computed from plate geometry. Real rolled sections carry root and toe radii, and IS channel flanges are tapered, so a real section weighs more than this page will tell you. Measured against IS 808:

  • ISA 50 × 50 × 6 angle — 4.427 against 4.50 kg/m, 1.6% light
  • ISMB 200 beam — 24.939 against 25.4 kg/m, 1.8% light
  • ISMC 100 channel — 9.024 against 9.56 kg/m, 5.6% light

The channel is the one to watch. If you are ordering a standard designation, take the weight from the mill's section table; use this calculator for a section that has no table entry, or for a cut-to-size fabricated profile.

Where the densities come from

A weight calculator is only as good as the density behind it, and most of them will not say where theirs came from. This one splits its materials into two tiers and tells you which one you are using every time you pick a material.

Mill-sourced grades

The 101 nickel, titanium, duplex, cobalt, nichrome and special stainless grades are the ones this business actually stocks. Their densities are read from the producing mill's own technical bulletin — Special Metals for INCONEL, INCOLOY, MONEL and NIMONIC, Haynes International for HASTELLOY and HAYNES, VDM Metals, Alleima, Outokumpu, ATI and voestalpine BÖHLER for the rest — and the calculator names the document under the figure. They come from the same verified grade database that writes the identity and chemistry tables on every product page on this site, so a density here cannot drift from the one printed there.

Four grades carry no density, because the bulletin publishes none: Haynes 214, Titanium Grade 6, Titanium Grade 23 and Kovar. They are still in the list, flagged, asking you for the figure. Inventing a plausible number would have been easy and would have been worse.

Generic materials

Mild steel, the 300 and 400 series stainless grades, the aluminium and copper alloys, tantalum, molybdenum, tungsten and the rest are nominal handbook values. Pure elements are CRC Handbook figures and are not in dispute; the alloy values are representative, and a real temper or heat will sit within about one per cent of them. That is fine for estimating and specifying, and it is not a substitute for the density on your own material certificate — which you can type straight over the top.

Questions

Is this the weight that will be invoiced?

No. This is a theoretical weight — nominal density on an ideal shape. Rolling, wall and diameter tolerances move the real figure by a few per cent, and material is invoiced on weighed shipping weight. Use this to budget, compare and specify, then ask us for a quotation for the number that will actually be billed.

How do I find my grade in the list?

Two filters sit above the material picker and they work together. Filter by family narrows the list to one alloy family or one class of common material — Inconel, Titanium, Stainless Steel, Copper Alloys — which brings the longest list down to seventeen. Search matches the grade name, the family and the identifiers, so 625, N06625 and 2.4856 all find Inconel 625. That matters when you are working from a drawing, where the UNS or Werkstoff number is usually the only name the material has.

Why does the calculator show kg per metre and kg per m²?

Because that is the figure most orders are actually quoted against. Bars, pipes and sections get kg per metre; sheet, plate, discs and rings get kg per square metre. For a coil entered by outside and inside diameter it also works out the developed strip length, which is usually what a strip buyer is after.

Can I share a calculation?

The material, form and unit are carried in the address bar, so a link such as /tools/weight-calculator/?material=inconel-625&shape=round-bar opens the calculator set up for Inconel 625 round bar.
