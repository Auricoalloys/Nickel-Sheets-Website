Such failures underscore the demands on materials in oil & gas, where Inconel® tubes provide unmatched oxidation and stress-corrosion cracking resistance up to 980°C.

Specifications and Standards

Inconel® tubes adhere to ASTM B163/B167 (seamless), B516/B517 (welded) for grades like UNS N06625, with ASME SB equivalents for pressure applications. DIN 2.4856 and EN 10095 ensure international compatibility, including NDT, flattening, and hydrostatic tests for marine and power sectors.

Equivalent Grades

Common grades: Inconel 625 (UNS N06625, W.Nr. 2.4856), 718 (UNS N07718, 2.4668, EN NiCr19Fe19NbMo3), 600 (UNS N06600, 2.4851). These match ISO NA21N or JIS NW4400, streamlining sourcing for procurement heads.

Product Forms and Availability

Available as seamless (OD 3-127mm, lengths to 12m), welded (up to 1524mm OD, Sch 10-80), suited for umbilicals and heat exchangers. Inconel hex bar complements for forged fittings in OEM assemblies.

Chemical Composition

Inconel 625: Ni balance (min 58%), Cr 20-23%, Mo 8-10%, Nb 3.15-4.15%, Fe 5% max, C 0.1% max. 718 adds Ti/Al for precipitation hardening; 600 has higher Fe (6-10%). Optimized for weldability and pitting resistance.

Mechanical and Physical Properties

625 yields 414 MPa min, tensile 827 MPa, elongation 30%; density 8.44 g/cm³, melt 1290-1350°C. Thermal conductivity 9.8-17.2 W/m-K, expansion 12.8-17.4 × 10⁻⁶/K. Maintains strength at 650°C.

Applications by Industry

Oil & gas deploys Inconel tubes for downhole and flare systems, resisting sour corrosion better than duplex steels; chemical processing handles oxidizing acids. Marine for propeller shafts; pharma for sterile processing; power for gas turbines.

The nickel alloys market, including Inconel, reached $18B in 2025, with oil & gas at 35% share.
