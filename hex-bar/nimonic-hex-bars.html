Such failures highlight the demand for robust superalloys like Nimonic hex bars, designed for superior creep resistance and oxidation endurance in turbine and power applications.

Specifications and Standards

Nimonic hex bars adhere to standards like ASTM B637/ASME SB637, UNS N07080 (Nimonic 80A), DIN/Werkstoff 2.4952 (80A), 2.4630 (263), BS HR5/NA20 (80A), and EN 2.4952. These ensure high cleanliness via VIM/ESR remelting for OEM and fabricators. Sizes range from 2mm to 100mm hex A/F, up to 500mm round equivalent.

Compliance includes mill certifications and NDT testing for export readiness from Mumbai.

Chemical Composition

Nimonic alloys emphasize nickel-chromium with Al/Ti for precipitation hardening. Nimonic 80A: Ni bal., Cr 18-21%, Ti 2.0-2.7%, Al 1.0-1.8%, Fe 3% max. Nimonic 75: Cr 18-21%, Fe 5% max; 263 adds Mo 5.6-6.1%, Co 19-21%.

Mechanical and Physical Properties

Nimonic 80A: Tensile 1000 MPa min, 0.2% yield 620 MPa, 20% elongation, hardness 300 HV. Creep-rupture strength to 815°C, density 8.19 g/cm³, melting 1374-1420°C. Excellent up to 920°C for 90.

Specific gravity supports lightweight turbine designs with high thermal fatigue resistance.

Frequently Asked Questions

What standards cover Nimonic hex bars?

Nimonic hex bars comply with ASTM B637, UNS N07080 (80A), DIN 2.4952, BS NA20/HR5. These validate high-temp properties for turbines via VIM/ESR processes. MTRs confirm for fabricators.

What are Nimonic 80A mechanical properties?

Tensile 1000 MPa min, yield 620 MPa, 20% elongation, 300 HV hardness; creep to 815°C. Density 8.19 g/cm³ suits aero/power apps. Outperforms SS in sustained heat.

Why use Nimonic in gas turbines?

Exceptional creep/oxidation resistance prevents fatigue failures common in hot sections. Grades like 90 excel to 920°C for blades/combustors, extending life vs standard alloys.

Available sizes for Nimonic hex bars?

Hex 2-100mm A/F, custom lengths; round to 500mm. Stock annealed/age-hardened. Pairs with Incoloy hex bars for diverse projects; Mumbai-sourced.
