In a petrochemical reformer, standard nickel alloy components oxidized rapidly at 1100°C, leading to tube rupture and a $2M shutdown—failures exacerbated by thermal fatigue where cobalt-based alloys excel. Haynes® hex bars address these challenges with unmatched oxidation resistance and fabricability for power generation and chemical processing.

Specifications and Standards

Haynes hex bars meet ASTM B572 (Haynes 25/UNS R30605), ASTM B435 (188/UNS R30188, 230/N06230), AMS 5772/5790, DIN 2.4683 (25), 2.4686 (188), EN 10095 equivalents. These ensure structural stability up to 1200°C for OEM turbine and furnace fabricators. Hex sizes 2-100mm A/F, lengths to 6m, with VIM/ESR cleanliness.

Full MTRs and NDT compliance support global procurement from Mumbai exporters.

Chemical Composition

Cobalt-based Haynes alloys prioritize oxidation resistance: Haynes 25 Co 50% bal., Cr 19-21%, W 14-16%, Fe 3% max, C 0.05-0.15%. Haynes 188 Ni 20-24%, La 0.03-0.15%; 230 adds Mo 1-3%, Nb 0.3-1%.

Mechanical and Physical Properties

Haynes 25: Tensile 1000 MPa, yield 520 MPa, 55% elongation at RT; retains 65 ksi at 980°C. Density 8.9 g/cm³ (25), thermal exp. 12.8 µm/m°C, conductivity 29 W/m°C. Excellent fabricability despite high strength.

Creep superior to nickel alloys up to 1150°C, vital for combustion zones.
