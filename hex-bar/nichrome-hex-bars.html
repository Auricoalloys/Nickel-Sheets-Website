In an industrial heat treatment furnace, heating elements failed prematurely due to oxidation and embrittlement after 6 months of cyclic operation, causing 30% of common furnace downtime and production losses exceeding $100k. Nichrome hex bars mitigate such issues with exceptional oxidation resistance and stable resistivity up to 1200°C.

Specifications and Standards

Nichrome hex bars align with UNS N06003 (80/20), DIN 17470/17471, 2.4869, EN 10095, ASTM B344 for wire but extended to bars via custom specs. These ensure consistent performance for OEM heating element fabricators. Sizes 3-50mm hex A/F, lengths to 3m, annealed condition.

Compliance includes resistivity testing and oxide scale verification for export from Mumbai.

Product Forms and Availability

Primarily hex, round (0.2-100mm), flat, strip for resistance elements; bars for structural supports. Readily available annealed or bright-drawn. Haynes alloy hex bars offer complementary high-temp strength for advanced furnace designs.

Chemical Composition

Nichrome 80: Ni bal., Cr 20-23%, Fe 1% max, Si 0.75-1.6%, C 0.08% max. NiCr 60/16: Ni 55-61%, Cr 15-18%, Fe bal. Chromium oxide layer enables longevity vs FeCrAl.

Mechanical and Physical Properties

Density 8.4 g/cm³, max temp 1200°C, resistivity 1.09 µΩ·m at 20°C, expansion 18x10-6 /°C. Hardness 140-150 HB, tensile ~600 MPa RT; retains ductility hot. Melting 1400°C, superior oxide adhesion.

Stable resistance minimizes element redesigns in furnaces.

Frequently Asked Questions

What standards govern Nichrome hex bars?

UNS N06003, DIN 2.4869/17470, EN 10095 for NiCr 80/20. Ensure stable resistivity (1.09 µΩ·m), oxidation to 1200°C. Ideal for furnace fabricators; verify MTRs.

Properties of Nichrome 80/20 hex bars?

Why Nichrome for heating elements?
